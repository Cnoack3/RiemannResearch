# The centered Pareto boundary and finite Gram determinants

Write `G_n` for the Pareto Gram matrix of the packets with conductors from 2 through
$n+1$, $\mu_i=\Lambda(i+2)$ for their means, $S_n=G_n-\mu\mu^\top$, and
$I_n=\mu^\top S_n^{-1}\mu$. The stage counts conductors. A cutoff on the number of
distinct prime factors is a different filtration. Let $u$ be the screened mean-one
vector, $r=1-P_V1$, and $d=\|\partial\|^2=\|u\|^2-1$ for the centered mean snake
boundary. All norms and projections are in the original Pareto geometry.

## Strict descent and the conductor-six orbit

Every finite mixed-conductor deficit strictly exceeds the limiting ambient boundary energy. Infinitely many one-step gains are positive. A ternary digit gives a squared-error lower bound of $49/1000$ for the entire closed dilation orbit of $J_6$. A rational trial in the full window through conductor 120 has squared error less than $39/1000$. The arithmetic period transform detects each finite fit and gives its leading nonzero moment. ## The two split rows and the ambient boundary

Take the positive integers with Pareto mass $P(j)=1/[j(j+1)]$, and let $H=L^2(P;\mathbb R)$. Write $r_q(j)=j\bmod q$, $J_q=\sum_{e\mid q}\mu(q/e)r_e$, and $V=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{r_q:q\ge2\}$. The closed mixed space $M$ is generated by the $J_q$ with at least two distinct prime divisors. The Pareto mean $C(v)=\langle v,\mathbf1\rangle_P$ sends $J_q$ to $\Lambda(q)$. Screening gives $u=(I-P_M)J_2/\log2$, $C(u)=1$, and $(I-P_M)J_q=\Lambda(q)u$. Thus $V=M\oplus^\perp\mathbb Ru$. Write $Z=\mathbf1^\perp\subset H$ and $T(v)=v-C(v)\mathbf1$.

$$
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
0&\longrightarrow&M&\longrightarrow&V&\xrightarrow{C}&\mathbb R&\longrightarrow&0\\
&&\big\downarrow\iota&&\big\downarrow T&&\big\downarrow 0\\
0&\longrightarrow&Z&\longrightarrow&H&\xrightarrow{\mu_P}&\mathbb R&\longrightarrow&0
\end{array}
$$

The sections send $t$ to $tu$ and $t\mathbf1$, respectively. The retractions are $P_M|_V$ and $I-\mathbf1\mu_P$. The upper mean has kernel $M=V\cap Z$. Every legal section sends 1 to $u+m$ for a unique $m\in M$, so the screened section uniquely minimizes the norm. On $V/M$, the class with mean $t$ has quotient norm $\lvert t\rvert\,\|u\|$. The factorization of a bounded map $F:V\to Y$ annihilating $M$ is unique and sends $t$ to $tF(u)$. Its operator norm is $\|u\|\,\|F\|$.

Write $\Delta=u-\mathbf1$, $L=V\cap\mathbb R\mathbf1$, and $S=T(V)=M\oplus^\perp\mathbb R\Delta$. The module snake lemma yields the exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow L\xrightarrow{C}\mathbb R\xrightarrow{\partial} Z/M
\xrightarrow{\eta} H/S\xrightarrow{\bar\mu_P}\mathbb R\longrightarrow0.
$$

A lift $v$ of $t$ gives $\partial(t)=[v-t\mathbf1]_M=t[\Delta]_M$. Changing the lift adds an element of $M$. The kernel of $\partial$ is $C(L)$, the kernel of $\eta([z]_M)=[z]_S$ is $\mathbb R[\Delta]_M$, and the kernel of $\bar\mu_P([h]_S)=\mu_P(h)$ is $Z/S$. The tail splits by $t\mapsto[t\mathbf1]_S$. The ranges are closed, so these descriptions use the ordinary Hilbert quotient norms. The exact-sequence assertion is in real modules.

Inclusion gives $\Phi:Z/M\simeq H/V$, $\Phi([z]_M)=[z]_V$. Its inverse sends $[h]_V$ to $[h-\mu_P(h)u]_M$. The identity $v-C(v)u=P_Mv\in M$ makes the inverse independent of representative. Under this equivalence, $\Phi\partial(t)=-t[\mathbf1]_V$. With $E=\|\mathbf1-P_V\mathbf1\|^2$, the two distinguished squared norms are $\|\partial(1)\|^2=E/(1-E)$ and $\|\Phi\partial(1)\|^2=E$. The internal quotient $(V\cap Z)/M$ is zero at every stage of the argument. Vanishing of the ambient class is equivalent to $u=\mathbf1$ and implies RH. If $\mathbf1\in V$, differences of scaled dilations of $\mathbf1$ give every point mass, so $V=H$, $M=Z$, and the entire ambient quotient vanishes.

## Descent through the support quotients

For any linear subspace $S\subseteq M$, take the mean rows
$0\to M/S\to V/S\to\mathbb R\to0$ and
$0\to Z/S\to H/S\to\mathbb R\to0$. Both split as real modules.
Write $T_S$ for the induced centering map. The stage diagram has its own
snake map $\partial_S(t)=t[[u-\mathbf1]_S]_{M/S}$. Under the third
isomorphism $(Z/S)/(M/S)\cong Z/M$ it becomes $\partial$. The same
identification holds along every transition $S\subseteq S'\subseteq M$.
The endpoint identifications $\ker T_S\cong V\cap\mathbb R\mathbf1$ and
$(H/S)/T_S(V/S)\cong H/T(V)$ intertwine the adjacent maps. These are the
statements of `Stage.supportDiagramTransformation`, `Stage.splittings_natural`,
`Stage.connectingTransformation`, `Stage.longSequence_exact`, `Stage.longSequence_comparison`,
`Stage.connecting_natural` and `Stage.connecting_one_eq_zero_iff`. [Stage snake](RH/CenteredMeanStageSnake.lean)

Let $J=\bigcup_s I_s$, where $I_s$ is the ideal of the closed support stage.
In real algebras the colimit of $V/I_s$ is $V/J$. Its canonical surjection
to $V/M\cong\mathbb R$ has kernel $M/J=\overline{\{0\}}$.
The union is dense in $M$. Each stage is a proper closed subspace of the
complete mixed space, so Baire's theorem supplies an element of $M$ outside
every stage. In Hausdorff real topological modules the colimit of the
ambient quotients $H/I_s$ is $H/M$. [Algebraic colimit and kernel](RH/AlgebraicSupportColimit.lean) · [Hausdorff colimit](RH/HausdorffSupportColimit.lean)

### The mixed bimodule and the category of the splitting

For $v\in V$ and $m\in M$ the two actions are
$v\star m=C(v)m=m\star v$. Lean equips the original Hilbert subspace $M$ with
commuting continuous left $V$ and right $V$ actions. They factor through
$V/I_s$ at every stage and through $V/M$. Both actions are compatible with the
tower transitions. The annihilator of $M$ in $V$ is exactly $M$, since the
nonzero packet $J_6$ detects the scalar $C(v)$. At stage $s$ the annihilator is
the kernel of $V/I_s\to V/M$. These computations use the module idealization
product of Anderson and Winders. [Square-zero algebra](RH/MixedConductorSquareZero.lean) · [Bimodule and annihilator](RH/MixedConductorBimodule.lean) · [Stage actions](RH/PrimeSupportAlgebraDescent.lean) · [Anderson and Winders (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1216/JCA-2009-1-1-3)

The regular $V$-module sequence $0\to M\to V\to V/M\to0$ is exact. Every
$V$-linear map $s:V/M\to V$ takes values in $M$. Indeed $m\star s(q)=s(mq)=0$
for every $m\in M$, so $s(q)$ belongs to the annihilator. In particular there is
no $V$-linear section. The continuous real-linear section of the mean and the
unital real-algebra section of its scalar quotient are the screened lift $t\mapsto tu$.

In coordinates $v=m+tu$, the screened multiplication is $(m,t)(m',t')=(tm'+t'm,tt')$. Its unit is $u$, and $M$ is a closed maximal ideal with $M^2=0$ and $V/M\simeq\mathbb R$ as algebras. The correspondence between real subspaces of $M$ and ideals inside $M$ is an order isomorphism and preserves closedness. Replacing $u$ by $u+m_0$ conjugates the multiplication by the shear $v\mapsto v+C(v)m_0$, fixes $M$ pointwise, and preserves the quotient class of the boundary.

For the CRT presentation, let $d_1,\ldots,d_j$ be the maximal prime-power factors of a mixed conductor $k$. Möbius inversion gives $r_k-\sum_i r_{d_i}=\sum_{q\mid k,\;\omega(q)\ge2}J_q$. The coefficient of $J_k$ is one, so induction on divisors gives the same mixed span from CRT carries and from packets. For squarefree $q$, the Ramanujan sum on the CRT product is $\prod_{p\mid q}(p\,\mathbf1_{p\mid n}-1)$. Pure prime powers survive screening as $(\log p)u$.

More generally, for a set of mixed conductors $A$ let $K_A$ be their closed packet span. The sequence $0\to K_A\to M\to K_A^{\perp_M}\to0$ splits by orthogonal projection. Its last term is the closed span of $(I-P_{K_A})J_q$ for mixed $q$ outside $A$. This follows by applying the projection to the dense packet span. [Lean block splittings](RH/MixedConductorBlockSplitting.lean)

Set $I_s=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{J_q:2\le\omega(q)\le s\}\subset M$, viewed inside either $V$ or $H$. Then $I_1=0$, the stages increase, and the closure of their union is $M$. For $s\le k$ the transitions on $V/I_s$ and $H/I_s$ send a representative to the same representative modulo $I_k$. They are surjective with kernel $I_k/I_s$. The Hilbert-space layer row splits orthogonally,
$$
0\to I_s\to I_{s+1}\to D_s\to0,\qquad
D_s=I_{s+1}\cap I_s^{\perp},\qquad P_{D_s}=P_{I_{s+1}}-P_{I_s}.
$$
The ordinary consecutive inclusions give the identity connecting map on $D_s$. Composing the middle inclusion with the previous-stage projection gives the zero connecting map for that comparison. The layer projections are mutually orthogonal and their energies sum to the squared norm on $M$. Any bounded section of the layer quotient is $x\mapsto x+A x$ with $Ax\in I_s$. Its squared norm at $x$ is $\|x\|^2+\|Ax\|^2$, so inclusion is the unique isometric section. A fresh squarefree conductor with $s+1$ prime divisors detects strict growth for $s\ge1$. In fact the new layer contains infinitely many independent vectors. Use squarefree conductors with exactly $s+1$ prime factors. Their Möbius coordinate functionals kill $I_s$, and each such functional singles out its own packet among this family because distinct squarefree conductors of the same support size cannot divide each other.

Compatible continuous linear maps from $H/I_s$ into a Hausdorff target factor uniquely through $H/M$. The analogous compatible algebra maps from $V/I_s$ factor through $V/M$ if the stage-zero leg is continuous and the target is Hausdorff. Indeed the common map on representatives kills every $I_s$, hence kills the closure $M$ by closedness of its kernel. Surjectivity of the quotient map gives uniqueness.

At the ambient vertex there is also a split short exact row $0\to Z/M\hookrightarrow H/M\xrightarrow{\bar\mu_P}\mathbb R\to0$. Its section sends $t$ to $[t\mathbf1]_M$. The snake target is thus the entire kernel of the descended ambient mean inside the vertex of the support tower.

The stage boundary is the composite $V/I_s\to V/M\simeq\mathbb R\xrightarrow{\partial}Z/M$. For any representative $v$, its image in $H/V$ is $-C(v)[\mathbf1]_V$. Consequently the kernel on representatives is exactly $M$ when $[\mathbf1]_V\ne0$, and is all of $V$ when $[\mathbf1]_V=0$. The legal unit and the class of $J_2/\log2-\mathbf1$ in $H/I_s$ have equal images in $H/M$. This last equality takes place after descent.

## The finite calculations and the quantified RH implication

For a finite mixed-conductor window through $B$, put $a=\log2$, $K_B=\mathrm{span}\{J_q:q\le B,\ \omega(q)\ge2\}$, $v_B=(I-P_{K_B})J_2/a$, and $D(B)=\|(I-P_{K_B})(J_2-a\mathbf1)\|^2$. The exact finite error is

$$
D(B)/a^2=\|\partial\|^2+\|v_B-u\|^2.
$$

The common multiple of a finite dictionary gives $D(B)>0$ for every $B$. As $B\to\infty$, $v_B\to u$. The explicit trial $y=(-4J_6-5J_{10}+4J_{12}-5J_{14}+3J_{15})/50$ has $50(J_2-1/2-y)$ of period 420 and absolute value at most 91. The rational 900-term squared-norm sum plus the full remainder bound $(91/50)^2/901$ is less than $11/60$. Thus $\|P_{K_{15}}J_2\|^2>1/15$, giving $D(15)<(11/16)(a-a^2)$. A support stage $I_s$ can contain infinitely many conductors. Its parity class has squared norm $Q_s=\|[J_2/a-\mathbf1]_{I_s}\|^2\to\|\partial\|^2$. For any fixed $0<c<1$, boundary vanishing is equivalent to finding $m\ge s$ with $Q_m\le cQ_s$ at arbitrarily late starting stages $s$.

A second finite test uses all packets $J_2,\ldots,J_{n+1}$, including prime powers. Let $G_n$ be their raw Pareto Gram matrix, $\lambda_i=\Lambda(i+2)$, $b_i=\mu(i+2)$, and solve $G_nc_n=\lambda$. Define
$$
F_n=-b^\top c_n+(2-\sqrt2)(1-\lambda^\top c_n).
$$
For every support stage $s$, its unit has zero ambient boundary if and only if for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every $N$ some $n\ge N$ satisfies $F_n\ge1-\varepsilon$. Equivalently, $\limsup_n F_n\ge1$. The determinant form follows by multiplying by the positive raw Gram determinant and applying the rank-one determinant identity. The one-solve form removes the determinants from the arithmetic obligation. The forward implication from this cofinal condition to RH is checked without a converse hypothesis. The repository theorem expressing the reverse implication from RH names the Báez–Duarte natural-approximation implication explicitly.

The rational interval calculations below include all Pareto sites. Wolfram digamma evaluations give the same signs. The exact interval computation below groups each pair of packets by its own period. For each residue it bounds the complete tail, encloses the Gram matrix and mean vector entrywise, and propagates those bounds through a single rational matrix inverse. For a residue progression of period $p$ whose first omitted term is $t$, the omitted mass lies between $1/(pt)$ and $1/[t(t+1)]+1/[p(t+1)]$. The script encloses the pairwise Gram entries with these full tails. Writing $G=A+\Delta$, $|\Delta|\le U$ entrywise and $|\lambda-\lambda_0|\le e$, it checks $\beta=\||A^{-1}|U\|_\infty<1$. The rational midpoint solve $c_0=A^{-1}\lambda_0$ then satisfies $\|c-c_0\|_\infty\le\||A^{-1}|(e+U|c_0|)\|_\infty/(1-\beta)$. This inequality follows by subtracting the two normal equations and bounding $\Delta(c-c_0)$ componentwise. Here $n$ counts packets, so stage 4 uses conductors 2 through 5.

```python
"""Enclose the first-site Pareto Gram score with exact rational arithmetic.

The stages use packets with conductors 2 through n + 1. The auxiliary row
with index 1 is the constant function. A pair of packet rows has period
equal to the least common multiple of its conductors.
"""
from functools import cache
from math import lcm

from sympy import Matrix, Rational, ceiling, divisors, floor, mobius

GRID = 10**20
PERIODS = 1024
STAGES = (2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 12)


def ceil_div(numerator, denominator):
    return -(-numerator // denominator)


@cache
def weight_intervals(period):
    """Integer bounds for GRID times each complete progression mass."""
    bounds = []
    for residue in range(1, period + 1):
        first_omitted = residue + period * PERIODS
        prefix = sum(
            GRID // (k * (k + 1))
            for k in range(residue, first_omitted, period)
        )
        t = first_omitted
        lower = prefix + GRID // (period * t)
        upper = (
            prefix + PERIODS + ceil_div(GRID, t * (t + 1))
            + ceil_div(GRID, period * (t + 1))
        )
        bounds.append((lower, upper))
    return bounds


@cache
def packet_row(conductor):
    if conductor == 1:
        return (1,)
    coefficients = [(int(d), int(mobius(conductor // d))) for d in divisors(conductor)]
    return tuple(
        sum(m * (k % d) for d, m in coefficients)
        for k in range(conductor)
    )


@cache
def gram_interval(q, h):
    """Return the midpoint and radius for the complete Pareto inner product."""
    left, right = packet_row(q), packet_row(h)
    lower = upper = 0
    for k, (low_weight, high_weight) in enumerate(weight_intervals(lcm(q, h)), 1):
        value = left[k % q] * right[k % h]
        lower += min(value * low_weight, value * high_weight)
        upper += max(value * low_weight, value * high_weight)
    return Rational(lower + upper, 2 * GRID), Rational(upper - lower, 2 * GRID)


def score_interval(n):
    gram, uncertainty = (
        Matrix(n, n, lambda i, j: gram_interval(min(i, j) + 2, max(i, j) + 2)[component])
        for component in (0, 1)
    )
    mean, mean_error = (
        Matrix([gram_interval(1, q)[component] for q in range(2, n + 2)])
        for component in (0, 1)
    )
    inverse = gram.inv()
    inverse_abs = inverse.applyfunc(abs)
    coefficients = inverse * mean
    coefficients_abs = coefficients.applyfunc(abs)
    perturbation = inverse_abs * uncertainty
    beta = max(sum(perturbation.row(i)) for i in range(n))
    if beta >= 1:
        raise ArithmeticError(f"Inverse enclosure failed at stage {n}")
    solve_error = max(inverse_abs * (mean_error + uncertainty * coefficients_abs)) / (1 - beta)

    left = Rational(58578643762690495119, GRID)
    right = Rational(58578643762690495120, GRID)
    if not (0 < left < right < 2 and (2 - left)**2 > 2 > (2 - right)**2):
        raise ArithmeticError("Invalid enclosure for 2 - sqrt(2)")
    slope, slope_error = (left + right) / 2, (right - left) / 2
    moebius = Matrix([mobius(q) for q in range(2, n + 2)])
    complement = 1 - mean.dot(coefficients)
    score = -moebius.dot(coefficients) + slope * complement
    radius = (
        slope_error * abs(complement)
        + right * mean_error.dot(coefficients_abs)
        + solve_error * sum((moebius + slope * mean).applyfunc(abs)
                            + slope_error * mean.applyfunc(abs) + right * mean_error)
    )
    return score - radius, score + radius


if __name__ == "__main__":
    denominator = 100_000
    for stage in STAGES:
        lower, upper = score_interval(stage)
        print(stage, floor(denominator * lower), ceiling(denominator * upper), denominator)
```

## A projection-free arithmetic obligation

For every $\varepsilon>0$ and cutoff $N$, it suffices to find $n\ge N$ satisfying

$$
 (2-\varepsilon)\det G_n\ \le\ \det A_n(1)+(2-\sqrt2)\det S_n .
$$

`boundary_eq_zero_iff_first_site_det_budget` proves that this quantifier statement is
equivalent to the vanishing of the actual boundary. Here $A_n(1)=G_n-\mu\,\mu_{\rm Mob}^{\top}$, where $\mu_{\rm Mob}$ is the vector of Möbius values. No full mixed projection occurs in this test. The coefficient comes from the dyadic residual estimate. The sharper sufficient condition takes $\varepsilon=0$
along arbitrarily late stages. The theorem `first_two_det_certificates` proves strict slack at stages one and two.
An estimate relating arbitrarily late determinants remains open.

For an exact implementation, one solve $G_n c_n=\mu$ supplies both scalars.
The zero-slack comparison is
$-\mu_{\rm Mob}^{\top}c_n+(2-\sqrt2)(1-\mu^\top c_n)\ge1$.
Other site values use $j_k^\top c_n$ from the same solve. The declaration `riemannHypothesis_of_cofinal_first_site_solve` accepts this single-solve test directly.

There is a different sign test at physical sites. For every fixed $k\ge1$, require
$\det A_n(k)\ge\det G_n$ at arbitrarily late $n$. The witness $n$ may depend on $k$
and on the requested cutoff. If the boundary is nonzero, then for every $B$ there
is a fixed site where eventually $\det A_n(k)<-B\det G_n$.
`boundary_eq_zero_iff_cofinal_site_det_sign` and
`eventually_negative_site_det_of_boundary_ne` give these two statements.

A finite lattice shortcut also fails already for conductors two and three. The vector $J_2-J_3$ is legal at that stage. ## The categorical and analytic steps

The split legal mean identifies its quotient with the screened line and leaves
the weight $\|u\|$ on the scalar coordinate. For $v\in V$, $m=P_Mv$, the existing
`legal_support_parseval` and `legalSupportEquiv` give
$\|v\|^2=|C(v)|^2\|u\|^2+\sum_k\|P_{L_k}m\|^2$ and the linear isometry onto
the screened line together with the Hilbert $\ell^2$ sum of the layers.
The natural comparison of successive
support rows has connecting map equal to the identity on the relevant layer.
The comparison through the stage retraction kills the layer and has zero boundary.
These are `successiveLayerBoundary_eq_id` and `truncatedLayerBoundary_eq_zero`.
In the ambient diagram the missing assertion is the vanishing of the class
of $u-1$ in $1^\perp/M$. The general snake map depends on all three vertical maps.
[Weibel, *An Introduction to Homological Algebra*, Lemma 1.3.2](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805214/35000/sample/9780521435000ws.pdf)
and [Stacks Project, Tag 07JV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07JV).

Bagchi gives the discrete Hilbert-space formulation and the Gram determinant
description of the finite distance. The logarithmic rate discussed on page 146
is conjectural. The calculations above require no asymptotic rate.
[Bagchi, *Proc. Indian Acad. Sci. (Math. Sci.)* 116 (2006), 137–146](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02829783).

In the abstract Hilbert model $H=\mathbb R^2\oplus\ell^2$, take $1=e_1$,
$u=e_1+s e_2$, $M=\ell^2$, and $V=\mathbb Ru\oplus M$. The mean is inner
product with $e_1$. Every coordinate stage of $M$ splits orthogonally and Parseval
holds with scalar weight $\sqrt{1+s^2}$. Yet $e_1\in V$ exactly when $s=0$.
The arithmetic site and covariance tests supply the additional content needed in
the Pareto problem.
