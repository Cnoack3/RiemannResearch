## 1. Actual setting and indexing

Work in H_Q with Q(m)=1/(zeta(2)m^2), m>=1, and the actual masked squarefree
stage generated by g_d(m)=(m mod d)/d for 2<=d<=N+1.
P_N is the stage projection, r_N=1-P_N1, Bf(m)=f(m)/(m+1),
b_N=P_N B r_N, and K_N(n,m)=(P_N e_n)(m).
Inactive masked coefficients need not be unique.

Write beta_n=1/(n+1) and, for every physical cutoff H,
\[
 A_{N,n,H}=\sum_{1\le m\le H} Q(m)r_N(m)(\beta_m-\beta_n)K_N(n,m).
\]
Orthogonality gives the complete exact identity
\[
 b_N(n)=A_{N,n,H}+
 \langle \operatorname{tail}_H r_N,\,
 \operatorname{tail}_H(B-\beta_n)k_{N,n}\rangle_Q.
\]
The original lower-site response L_N(n) is A_{N,n,n-1}.
Lean uses evaluation index n+1 where this note writes physical n; finiteHead H
contains m<=H and tail H retains all m>H.

## 2. Formalized complete forcing-tail Bessel estimates

Let E_d=d B g_d-(1-B1). Support localization and the existing Bessel bound give
\[
 \boxed{\zeta(2)\sum_{d\ge D}|\langle r,E_d\rangle_Q|^2
 \le4\|\operatorname{tail}_{D-1}r\|_Q^2.}
\]
The left side is a complete infinite conductor sum. The same bound holds for
every finite subset of conductors >=D. The proof first replaces r by its
physical tail, since E_d vanishes below d, and then applies the established
full-family Bessel inequality. No inverse Gram estimate is used.

For any r,s in H_Q,
\[
 \zeta(2)\sum_{d\ge1}
 |\langle r,E_d\rangle_Q-\langle s,E_d\rangle_Q|^2
 \le4\|r-s\|_Q^2.
\]
Thus the forcing analysis is Lipschitz, and actual residual convergence implies
uniform tightness of the entire weighted forcing tail:
\[
 \forall\epsilon>0\ \exists D_0\ \forall D\ge D_0\ \forall N,\quad
 \zeta(2)\sum_{d\ge D}|d\langle r_N,Bg_d\rangle_Q
 -\langle r_N,1-B1\rangle_Q|^2\le\epsilon.
\]
This still controls the forcing before the normal solve.

## 3. Formalized relative-scale and shrinking-head localization

For C>=1, the original diagonal center obeys
\[
 n+1\le(C+1)(H+2)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{|b_N(n)-A_{N,n,H}|^2
 \le\zeta(2)C^2\|\operatorname{tail}_H r_N\|_Q^2.}
\]
The general multiplier theorem uses only that, on m>H,
0<=beta_m<=1/(H+2). If a<=c and 1/(H+2)<=a+c, c>=0, then
\[
 \|\operatorname{tail}_H(B-aI)f\|_Q^2\le c^2\|f\|_Q^2.
\]
Combine this with ||k_{N,n}||^2<=zeta(2)n^2 and Cauchy--Schwarz.
Choosing C=1 allows a cutoff around n/2 with the same zeta(2) constant as
the earlier upper-site estimate. Every fixed relative-scale complement
therefore vanishes uniformly in N.

A stronger, fully quantified theorem is now available:
for every eta,epsilon>0 and every absolute cutoff M, there exists n_0 such that
every n>=n_0 admits H with
\[
 M\le H\le\eta n,\qquad
 \sup_N|b_N(n)-A_{N,n,H}|^2\le\epsilon.
\]
This holds at every sufficiently large evaluation site, not just along a
subsequence. The proof chooses an integer j with eta*j>=1 and uses integer
division to take H near n/j after the uniform tail threshold is reached.

The full Schur identity can be localized at any H and any center a:
\[
 \Lambda_{N,n}=
 \|\operatorname{head}_H(B-aI)k_{N,n}\|^2+
 \|\operatorname{tail}_H(B-aI)k_{N,n}\|^2-
 \|P_N(B-aI)k_{N,n}\|^2.
\]
The new relative-tail upper estimate retains the negative projected square.

## 4. A single sublinear cutoff: ordinary mathematical corollary

This explicit selection is an ordinary mathematical deduction, not a separate
Lean declaration here. Set
\[
 t(H)=\sup_N\|\operatorname{tail}_H r_N\|_Q,\quad
 q(n)=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil,\quad
 h(n)=q(n)+\lceil n\sqrt{t(q(n))}\rceil.
\]
The supremum is finite (all residual norms are at most one), and uniform tail
tightness gives t(H) down to zero. Consequently h(n) tends to infinity and
h(n)/n tends to zero. Eventually h(n)<n. On m>h(n),
|beta_m-beta_n|<=1/(h(n)+2), so
\[
 \sup_N|b_N(n)-A_{N,n,h(n)}|
 \le\sqrt{\zeta(2)}\,\frac{n\,t(h(n))}{h(n)+2}
 \le\sqrt{\zeta(2)}\sqrt{t(q(n))}\longrightarrow0.
\]
When t(q(n))=0 the response is exactly zero. The cutoff is explicit in an
unknown tail modulus; it is not an effective arithmetic rate.

## 5. Arithmetic obstruction to a bare inverse-response bound

This section is an independently checked ordinary proof. The finite packet
formulas have Lean declarations, but the complete L1 summation, squarefree-pair
count, and final unboundedness assertion are not yet formalized.

Suppose d>=2 and d,d+1 are squarefree. At the actual stage N=d, put
\[
 f_d=d g_d-(d+1)g_{d+1}.
\]
This is a legal vector, vanishes for n<d, and f_d(d)=-d.
For n=q d+r, 1<=q<=d, 0<=r<d, residue division gives exactly
\[
 f_d(qd+r)=
 \begin{cases}q-d-1,&r<q,\\q,&r\ge q.\end{cases}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \sum_{r=0}^{d-1}|f_d(qd+r)|=q(2d+1-2q)\le2qd,\qquad
 \sum_{r=0}^{d-1}\frac{|f_d(qd+r)|}{(qd+r)^2}\le\frac2{qd}.
\]
Globally |f_d(n)|<=d. With H_d=sum_{q=1}^d 1/q and L=d(d+1),
the first d blocks and the complete remaining tail give
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|f_d(n)|}{n^2}
 \le\frac{2H_d}{d}+
 d\sum_{n\ge L}\frac1{n^2}
 \le\frac{2H_d}{d}+\frac d{L-1}
 \le\frac{2H_d+1}{d}.
\]
The middle inequality uses 1/n^2<=1/[n(n-1)] and telescopes.
Hence ||f_d||_{L1(Q)}<=(2H_d+1)/(zeta(2)d).

Let x be any normal-system solution synthesizing k_{d,d}; use e for active
conductors 2<=e<=d+1. Reproduction and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 d=|f_d(d)|=
 \left|\sum_e x_e\langle f_d,g_e\rangle_Q\right|
 \le\left(\sum_e e^2|\langle f_d,g_e\rangle_Q|^2\right)^{1/2}
     \left(\sum_e x_e^2/e^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Since |g_e|<=1 and there are at most d active conductors,
\[
 \boxed{\left(\sum_e x_e^2/e^2\right)^{1/2}
 \ge \frac{\zeta(2)d^{3/2}}{(d+1)(2H_d+1)}.}
\]
The right side grows like sqrt(d)/log(d). This holds for every normal solution;
inactive masked coefficients are irrelevant.

There are infinitely many consecutive squarefree pairs by an elementary sieve:
\[
 \sum_p p^{-2}\le\frac14+\frac19+
 \sum_{k\ge2}\frac1{(2k+1)^2-1}
 =\frac{35}{72}<\frac12.
\]
For 1<=d<=X, non-squarefree pairs are covered by divisibility of d or d+1 by p^2,
so their number is at most
\[
 \sum_p\left(\lfloor X/p^2\rfloor+\lfloor(X+1)/p^2\rfloor\right)
 \le(2X+1)\frac{35}{72}.
\]
Thus at least X/36-35/72 pairs survive; discarding d=1 still leaves infinitely
many. Combined with H_d<=1+log d, this disproves a uniform bound for the bare
coefficient-response norm in the actual squarefree system.

**Scope:** this rules out separating the Bessel estimate from its response
by a uniform bound on ||x_e/e||_2. It does not refute (L), (K), signed
forcing-response cancellation, or an error-weighted response estimate.
