# Ramanujan–Laguerre continuation: legal Schur repair and a bounded-variance target

Date: 2026-09-11. Ordinary mathematical research; no Lean was used.

This continues `RH_Legal_Sequences_Mobius_Flow_20260911.md` and the subsequent Ramanujan-basis discussion. It proves exact basis, moment, finite-feasibility, and averaging statements. It constructs a specific sequence of finite legal moment-matching optimizers. **A uniform variance bound for that sequence remains unproved. RH is not proved.** Numerical values below are exploratory floating-point calculations, not interval-certified inequalities.

## 1. Exact legal coordinates

Let

\[
R_d(n)=n\bmod d,\qquad g_{a,m}=R_{am}-R_m,
\quad a\in\{2,3\},\quad m\ge1.
\]

For d>1 define the integrated Ramanujan function

\[
B_d(n)=-\sum_{k=1}^n C_d(k)
      =\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)R_e(n),
\]

where

\[
C_d(n)=\sum_{e\mid(d,n)}e\mu(d/e).
\]

The classical divisor identity and finite Ramanujan orthogonality are discussed in [Haukkanen, *Discrete Ramanujan-Fourier Transform of Even Functions*](https://arxiv.org/html/1210.0295v1). The deductions below apply them to the present legal family.

Möbius inversion gives

\[
R_L=\sum_{d\mid L,\ d>1}B_d.
\]

Consequently a generator packet has exactly the earlier Jordan coefficients:

\[
F=\sum_{d>1}H_dB_d.
\]

If d=p^v u, p in {2,3}, v>=1, p does not divide u, then

\[
\boxed{B_d=\sum_{e\mid u}\mu(u/e)g_{p,p^{v-1}e}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Therefore the full two-seed legal space is exactly the finite span of B_d with 2|d or 3|d. These functions are independent: their integer differences are -C_d, and the distinct C_d are orthogonal on a common period. Thus arbitrary finite real coefficients on the allowed conductors are legal.

For the finite space generated by m<=M, an exact basis is

\[
\mathcal D_M=\{2,4,\ldots,2M\}\cup\{3,6,\ldots,3M\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

To prove the reverse inclusion at a finite cutoff, use p=2 in (1.1) when d is even and d<=2M; otherwise use p=3. Every dilation on the right is <=M. The dimension is

\[
|\mathcal D_M|=2M-\lfloor M/3\rfloor.
\]

All packets are real, finite, bounded, periodic, and satisfy the reflection identity in the source criterion.

## 2. The full Pareto Schur complement

Use P(N=n)=1/[n(n+1)]. The exact means are

\[
\lambda_d=\mathbb E B_d(N)=\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)\log e=\Lambda(d).
\]

Within the legal basis only 2-powers and 3-powers have nonzero means. Put those conductors in P and put all the remaining allowed conductors in C. Members of C have at least two distinct prime factors and mean zero.

Define the actual second-moment matrix

\[
K_{de}=\mathbb E[B_d(N)B_e(N)].
\]

It is positive definite on every finite set of distinct conductors, since all Pareto atoms have positive mass and the functions are independent.

For prime-power coefficients a, the exact optimal composite correction is

\[
t_*=-K_{CC}^{-1}K_{CP}a.
\]

Its variance, assuming \(\lambda_P^Ta=1\), is

\[
\boxed{a^TSa-1,\qquad S=K_{PP}-K_{PC}K_{CC}^{-1}K_{CP}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Optimizing a as well gives

\[
\min\operatorname{Var}(F)=\frac1{\lambda_P^TS^{-1}\lambda_P}-1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Every entry includes the full Pareto progression tails. Explicitly, for q=lcm(d,e),

\[
K_{de}=\sum_{r=1}^q W_q(r)B_d(r)B_e(r),
\qquad
W_q(r)=\frac{\psi((r+1)/q)-\psi(r/q)}q.
\tag{2.3}
\]

### A first repair with closed constants

Let L=log2 and A=pi/sqrt3. For B_2=R_2 and B_6=R_6-R_3-R_2, finite residue summation gives

\[
K_{22}=L,\qquad K_{26}=L-A/2,\qquad K_{66}=A,
\qquad \mathbb EB_6=0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Starting with F_0=B_2/L, the optimal correction using B_6 alone is

\[
\boxed{
F_*=\frac{B_2}{\log2}+
\left(\frac1{2\log2}-\frac{\sqrt3}{\pi}\right)B_6.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

This is a finite legal mean-one packet. Its variance is exactly

\[
\frac1L-1-\frac{(L-A/2)^2}{AL^2}.
\]

The starting variance is approximately 0.44269504, the repaired variance is 0.39026475, and the coefficient of B_6 is 0.17001863. Thus the negative Pareto covariance supplies a definite gain; uniform orthogonality would have missed it.

## 3. A basis that diagonalizes the actual Pareto norm

Introduce a continuous Pareto variable X with density x^{-2} on x>=1. Then

\[
N=\lfloor X\rfloor,qquad T=\log X\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1).
\]

The log moments below refer to X, through T=logX. This distinction is necessary for the displayed Mellin identities.

For a legal packet F define

\[
\widetilde F(t)=F(\lfloor e^t\rfloor).
\]

The ordinary Laguerre polynomials

\[
L_k(t)=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}\frac{t^j}{j!}
\]

are an orthonormal complete family for L^2([0,infinity),e^{-t}dt). The standard orthogonality conventions are recorded in [NIST DLMF, §18.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.3).

Set

\[
A_k(F)=\int_0^\infty\widetilde F(t)L_k(t)e^{-t}\,dt,
\qquad A_k(d)=A_k(B_d).
\]

Then A_0(F)=EF and, for mean-one F, Parseval gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(F(N))=\sum_{k\ge1}|A_k(F)|^2.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For F=sum_d H_d B_d this becomes

\[
\operatorname{Var}(F(N))=
\sum_{k\ge1}\left|\sum_d H_dA_k(d)\right|^2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The sum over d is finite. This is an exact transformation of the full Pareto norm: mixed terms between arithmetic conductors remain inside each square. It does not replace Pareto sampling by uniform sampling.

One elementary completeness proof is as follows. If h is orthogonal to all polynomials in L^2(e^{-t}dt), its Laplace transform \(\int h(t)e^{-t}e^{zt}dt\) is analytic for Re(z)<1/2 and has every derivative zero at z=0. It therefore vanishes identically, and uniqueness of the Laplace transform gives h=0.

## 4. Arithmetic cancellation according to the number of prime factors

For d>1, the Mellin transform of B_d is

\[
\mathcal M_d(s)=\int_1^\infty B_d(\lfloor x\rfloor)x^{-s-1}\,dx
=-\frac{\zeta(s)}s\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)e^{1-s}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The expression is analytic for Re(s)>0 after filling in the removable value at s=1. The identity follows initially from the finite remainder decomposition and then by analytic continuation.

Write r=omega(d), the number of distinct prime factors, and q=rad(d). With s=1+u, the finite exponential sum factors as

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{a\mid d}\mu(d/a)\exp(-u\log a)
=(d/q)^{-u}\prod_{p\mid d}(p^{-u}-1).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Formula (4.2) has a zero of order r at u=0. Since zeta has a simple pole with residue 1 there,

\[
\mathcal M_d(1+u)=
(-1)^{r+1}\left(\prod_{p\mid d}\log p\right)u^{r-1}+O(u^r).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The Laurent expansion and Euler–Maclaurin formulas for zeta are standard; see [NIST DLMF, §25.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2).

Differentiating under the integral is valid for each finite bounded packet. Thus

\[
\mathbb E[B_d(N)T^j]=0\quad(0\le j\le r-2),
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E[B_d(N)T^{r-1}]=(r-1)!\prod_{p\mid d}\log p.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
A_k(d)=0\quad(k<\omega(d)-1),
\qquad
A_{\omega(d)-1}(d)=(-1)^{\omega(d)-1}\prod_{p\mid d}\log p.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

For r=1 the range of zero coefficients is empty. This recovers the prime-power mean. For r=2 the mean is zero, and for r=3 both the mean and the first Laguerre coefficient are zero.

This triangular structure is the useful interaction between Ramanujan arithmetic and the Pareto law. It permits exact cancellation of successively higher Laguerre coefficients without changing the lower ones.

## 5. A rigid triangular construction, and why it is not the recommended sequence

Let d_k be the product of the first k+1 primes, so d_0=2, d_1=6, d_2=30, d_3=210, and so on. Begin with F_0=B_2/log2 and recursively set

\[
F_k=F_{k-1}-\frac{A_k(F_{k-1})}{A_k(d_k)}B_{d_k}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

By (4.4), this is finite, legal, mean-one, and

\[
A_j(F_k)=0\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]

The first coefficient is explicit:

\[
F_1=\frac{B_2}{\log2}
+\frac{\gamma-\tfrac12\log2}{(\log2)(\log3)}B_6.
\]

Its correction coefficient is approximately 0.30287862, whereas the variance-minimizing one-block coefficient in (2.5) is about 0.17001863. Exact moment cancellation and minimum variance are different finite requirements.

This does not prove divergence, but it gives a concrete reason not to prioritize this rigid choice. It moves error into the remaining Laguerre coefficients at increasing cost. It establishes neither a bounded norm nor vanishing variance.

## 6. A fully specified flexible sequence

For each K>=0, set M_K=2^K and use every B_d with d in D_{M_K}. Let G_K be their full Pareto second-moment matrix. Define the constraint matrix

\[
\mathsf A_K(j,d)=A_j(d),\qquad0\le j\le K,
\]

and let e_0=(1,0,...,0)^T. Define

\[
\boxed{
H^{(K)}=G_K^{-1}\mathsf A_K^T
\left(\mathsf A_KG_K^{-1}\mathsf A_K^T\right)^{-1}e_0,
\qquad F_K=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D_{2^K}}H_d^{(K)}B_d.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is an explicit sequence determined by finite matrices and ordinary special-function constants. It minimizes the full Pareto norm under

\[
\mathbb EF_K=1,\qquad A_j(F_K)=0\quad(1\le j\le K).
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof of feasibility for every K

The allowed basis contains B_{2^{v+1}} for v=0,...,K. Their Mellin transforms are

\[
\mathcal M_{2^{v+1}}(1+u)=2^{-vu}\mathcal M_2(1+u).
\]

For fixed j, the Laguerre coefficient A_j(2^{v+1}) is a polynomial in v of degree j, with leading coefficient

\[
\frac{\log2}{j!}(-\log2)^j\ne0.
\]

Evaluation at the distinct integers v=0,...,K gives an invertible polynomial evaluation matrix. Hence the rows of A_K are independent, the constraints are feasible, and every inverse in (6.1) exists. The extra composite conductors provide the freedom to reduce the cost.

The exact minimum variance is

\[
\boxed{
V_K=e_0^T
\left(\mathsf A_KG_K^{-1}\mathsf A_K^T\right)^{-1}e_0-1.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is a constrained extension of the Schur optimizer. It uses the full legal basis and the full Pareto norm.

## 7. A proved bounded-variance-to-approximation theorem

**Theorem.** Suppose F_K are finite legal packets satisfying (6.2), and suppose

\[
\sup_K\operatorname{Var}(F_K(N))\le B<\infty.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then there is a subsequence F_{K_j} such that the finite legal mean-one averages

\[
\overline F_m=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mF_{K_j}
\]

satisfy

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(\overline F_m(N))\le\frac Bm+\frac4{m^2}\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The RH implication in the supplied Pareto criterion therefore applies.

**Proof.** Write e_K=F_K-1 in the Pareto Hilbert space, represented in the exponential variable T. The errors are bounded by sqrt(B) in norm and have Laguerre coefficients zero in degrees 0,...,K. Completeness of the Laguerre basis implies e_K converges weakly to zero: approximate any fixed test vector by a finite Laguerre sum, against which the inner product is eventually zero, and bound the remaining inner product using sqrt(B).

Choose K_j successively so that

\[
|\langle e_{K_i},e_{K_j}\rangle|\le2^{-j}\qquad(i<j).
\]

Weak convergence guarantees this simultaneous finite collection of requirements. Expanding the norm of the average gives

\[
\left\|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m e_{K_j}\right\|^2
\le\frac{B}{m}+\frac2{m^2}\sum_{j=2}^m(j-1)2^{-j}
\le\frac{B}{m}+\frac4{m^2}.
\]

Every average is a finite linear combination of legal generators and retains mean one. No independence assumption is made, and every mixed term is accounted for in the inner-product bound. QED.

The subsequence is essential to this argument; weak convergence alone does not guarantee strong convergence of the full sequence of ordinary Cesàro means.

**Open target.** Prove a uniform bound for (6.3), with the explicitly prescribed M_K=2^K, or construct another finite dilation schedule with such a bound. Even a deliberately loose estimate such as V_K<=1 for all K would suffice. This bound is not established by the numerical table and is not an automatic consequence of moment matching.

## 8. Where an off-line zero would obstruct the bound

The Laguerre generating function yields

\[
\mathcal A_F(z):=\sum_{k\ge0}A_k(F)z^k
=\frac1{1-z}\mathcal M_F\left(\frac1{1-z}\right),\qquad |z|<1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

This equality follows first in a neighborhood of zero by differentiation or integration of the generating function, then throughout the disk by analyticity. The disk corresponds to Re(s)>1/2. For an individual Ramanujan block,

\[
\mathcal A_{B_d}(z)
=-\zeta\left(\frac1{1-z}\right)
\sum_{a\mid d}\mu(d/a)\exp\!\left(-\frac{z\log a}{1-z}\right).
\tag{8.2}
\]

The apparent singularity at z=0 is removable. Parseval identifies the coefficient square sum with the H^2 norm and with the actual Pareto norm.

Suppose rho is a nontrivial zeta zero with Re(rho)>1/2. Put

\[
z_\rho=1-1/\rho,\qquad r=|z_\rho|<1.
\]

Every finite legal packet has A_F(z_rho)=0. If its mean is one and its next K coefficients vanish, then

\[
\mathcal A_F(z)-1=z^{K+1}H(z),\qquad
\|H\|_{H^2}^2=\operatorname{Var}(F(N)).
\]

At z=z_rho, H(z_rho)=-z_rho^{-(K+1)}. The elementary H^2 point-evaluation bound, obtained by Cauchy–Schwarz on the power series, gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(F(N))\ge(1-r^2)r^{-2(K+1)}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Thus an off-line zero would force exponential growth in the cost of exact moment matching, regardless of the chosen finite dilation range. The open uniform bound would exclude this obstruction; it does not assume it away. A similar argument using the artificial zeros of the 2-seed Mellin factor explains why the dyadic witnesses used merely to prove finite feasibility cannot themselves stay uniformly bounded as K grows.

## 9. Numerical scope and remaining work

The Schur experiments used pairwise periodic digamma sums and an exact integer change from generator coordinates to the independent B_d basis. All infinite progression tails were represented in those formulas. Mean and Schur consistency residuals in the displayed runs were at floating-point precision.

The moment experiments evaluated finite Taylor coefficients through the zeta Laurent expansion, using long-double Euler–Maclaurin arithmetic. Two cutoffs, N=200 and N=1000, agreed through the first seven coefficients to about 2.6e-17. The optimizer calculations and variance evaluations then used floating-point linear algebra. These checks support exploratory arithmetic consistency; they are not uniform estimates or interval certificates.

Established: independent legal coordinates; exact finite Schur repair; the Laguerre Parseval identity for the full Pareto norm; cancellation by omega(d); feasibility and a formula for every finite optimizer F_K; and the theorem turning a uniform variance bound into legal strong approximation by subsequence averages.

Still open: a bound on V_K uniform in K, or another explicit moment-matching family with bounded variance. The rigid one-conductor-per-moment construction has unfavorable finite behavior and should not be the default. The full constrained Schur optimizer is the concrete candidate to study next. No mathematical novelty claim is made without a broader literature comparison.
