# Isolation from the 21-term window's energy and finite-frame certificate

For every fixed $0<c\le10^{-5}$ the full-array, two-neighbor isolation argument gives
$$
 \liminf_{T\to\infty} B_0(T,c)/N(T)\ \ge\ 0.67385114
$$
using the finite-frame certificate for the 21-term cosine window $J_{21}$ in rStation paper 151 v2 and the independent energy enclosure in paper 155. The input papers are research-platform manuscripts. The angular calculation here works for every even unit-mass probability density with the indicated energy, using just the energy enclosure in place of the individual coefficients. The corresponding repository result using the pinned Lean ten-point certificate gives $0.673637381$. Kim's preprint gives $0.672468$ for this same statistic. ## An angular bound uniform over the energy ball

Let $I=[-1/2,1/2]$, $f_0(t)=\cos(\sqrt2 t)/(\sqrt2\sin(1/\sqrt2))$, and $M_0(y)=\int_I f_0(t)\cosh(2\pi yt)\,dt$. For an even density $f$ set $h=f-f_0$. Its integral is zero. By stationarity, the excess $C(f)-C(f_0)$ is precisely $\mathcal E(h,h)$ for the pair-energy form in the variational note. In the cosine expansion $h=\sum_{j\ge1}c_j\cos(2\pi jt)$, orthogonality and the integrated cosine identity give
$$
 C(f)-C(f_0)
   =\frac12\sum_{j\ge1}\left(1-\frac1{2\pi^2j^2}\right)c_j^2
   \ge \left(1-\frac1{2\pi^2}\right)\lVert h\rVert_2^2.
$$
Finite cosine expansions are dense in even zero-mean $L^2(I)$, and $\mathcal E$ is continuous. Paper 155 gives $C(J_{21})<1.3280961313771574$, hence $\lVert J_{21}-f_0\rVert_2<R=0.025074$. [Variational calculation](VariationalIsolation20261119.md) · [Paper 155, §10](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/155)

Write $U(y)=\sinh(\pi y)/(\pi y)$, extended by continuity at zero, and $k_y(t)=\cosh(2\pi yt)$. Then
$$
 V(y,z)=\langle k_y-U(y),k_z-U(z)\rangle
       =\{U(y+z)+U(y-z)\}/2-U(y)U(z).
$$
For $y$ in a rational box $[v,w]$, let $q=\min(1,\ell(v-a)_+)$, $u=2-\sqrt{2-q}$, $d=1-u$, $p=2w$, and $\alpha=4M_0(v)$. The smaller eigenvalue of the matrix with diagonal $(1+M_f(2y),1)$ and off-diagonal $\sqrt2M_f(y)$ is at most $u$ if
$$
 2M_f(y)^2 \ge d(1+M_f(2y)-u).
$$
Both Laplace transforms are increasing, so it suffices to put $v$ into the left transform and $p$ into the right transform. Expand around $f_0$, keep the nonnegative square, and use Cauchy–Schwarz on the one remaining signed linear functional. Its test vector, after centering, is $\alpha(k_v-U(v))-d(k_p-U(p))$. A sufficient condition is
$$
 2M_0(v)^2-d(1+M_0(p)-u)
 >R\sqrt{\alpha^2 V(v,v)+d^2 V(p,p)-2\alpha d V(v,p)}.
$$
For $a=1.425$ and $\ell=0.7381$, directed arithmetic proves this strict inequality on 7,393 rational intervals covering $[0,a+1/\ell]$, with maximum depth 19. Thus the clipped weak price is at most $\ell(y-a)_+$ everywhere. Beyond the cover the universal bound $g(w)-2\le1$ suffices. Strict compact margins and convergence of the mollified Laplace transforms preserve the estimates in the smoothing limit. [Directed replay](BoundCertificateReplays20261119.md#verify_j21_isolationpy)

## Transfer of the source score

The finite-frame certificate of paper 151 applies to every finite set of real coordinates, in particular the isolated subset. It supplies $(2-C-b)/(1-x)\ge 0.67385328759554148395$ and $0\le x<0.013$. The clipped trace inequality and the symmetric Conrey stop-loss give
$$
 \liminf\frac{B_0(T,c)}{N(T)}
 \ge 0.67385328759554148395
       -\frac{\ell F(2\pi a)/(4\pi)+4\pi^2\,10^{-10}}{1-0.013}
 >0.67385114254329086.
$$
Here $F$ is the profile in the Conrey note. The analytic frame and stop-loss assumptions have the same scope as in the ten-point transfer. Paper 179's stronger $0.67385611$ concerns simple critical-line zeros. Its ordered-moment and capacity inputs involve the actual full simple source, so transfer of that number to isolated subsets requires an additional argument.
