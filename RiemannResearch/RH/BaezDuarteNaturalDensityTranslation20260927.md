## Exact correspondence

In the paper, H = L²((0,∞), dx), ρ_d(x) = {1/(d x)}, and χ = 1_(0,1].
Theorem 1.1 states RH if and only if χ belongs to the closed linear span
of ρ_d for positive integer d. It does not require arbitrary real dilations.
Real coefficients suffice: replacing complex coefficients by their real
parts cannot increase distance to the real target χ.

Set ψ_d = ρ_d − ρ_1/d. For x>0, put n=floor(1/x). Then exactly

    ψ_d(x) = {1/(d x)} − {1/x}/d = (n mod d)/d = g_d(n).

This is the existing theorem `pareto_nyman_pointwise`. In particular ψ_1=0
and ψ_d(x)=0 for x>1. For n≥1 the reciprocal cell
(1/(n+1),1/n] has length 1/(n(n+1)). The endpoint convention has no L² effect.
The zero coordinate has zero Pareto mass and all residue atoms vanish there.
Thus for every finite real coefficient family c,

    integral_(0,1] |1 − sum_d c_d ψ_d(x)|² dx
      = sum_(n≥1) |1 − sum_d c_d (n mod d)/d|² / (n(n+1)).

`nyman_finite_lintegral_error` already verifies this identity for the entire
nonnegative integral. The new bridge identifies it with the norm of the
existing `hilbertOne` minus an actual finite sum of
`normalizedResidueHilbert d hd`. The actual closed span is
`CoefficientStabilization.closedSpace`, generated by d=2,3,... .

## Why centering loses no approximation theorem

For F=sum_d a_d ρ_d, let A=sum_d a_d/d and G=F−Aρ_1=sum_d a_dψ_d.
For x>1, F(x)=A/x, whereas χ(x)=0. Therefore

    integral_1^∞ |F(x)−χ(x)|² dx = A².

Since 0≤ρ_1<1 on (0,1],

    ||G−χ||² ≤ 2 integral_0^1 |F−χ|² + 2 A² ≤ 2 ||F−χ||².

The Lean transfer uses the weaker universal constant 10: the finite interval
(1,2] gives A² ≤ 4 ||F−χ||², and the unit interval then gives
||G−χ||² ≤ 10 ||F−χ||². This avoids an additional improper-integral
formalization; either constant transfers arbitrarily small errors.

Consequently the paper's approximation property implies centered residue
approximation at every tolerance. Conversely, a centered combination is
itself an uncentered combination after adding its ρ_1 coefficient.
For conductors 2,...,N, the latter coefficient is −sum_(d=2)^N c_d/d.
There is no sign reversal in g_d; the familiar −μ(d) sign arises because
the paper's Möbius sums approximate −χ.

## Formal analytic boundary

The normalization and finite-error transfer are elementary. The classical
implication from Mathlib's `RiemannHypothesis` to natural-dilation
approximation is a separate, substantive analytic theorem.

The paper proves it using ordered Möbius Dirichlet sums and their uniform
vertical bounds (Littlewood and Balazard–Saias, Lemma 2.1), a Mellin
Plancherel isometry, the fractional-part Mellin identity, functional equation
and Gamma estimates (Lemma 2.2), and RH-implied Lindelöf bounds. An identity
or a pointwise limit does not supply the required Hilbert norm limit.
Báez-Duarte's weighted route uses a symmetric shifted-zeta quotient from
his Lemma 2.2; it does not require the separate Hadamard-quotient route
listed in the historical Bagchi audit or a general Hardy converse.
The default branch's strengthened unconditional endpoint estimates do not
provide the needed fixed small positive dampings tending to zero.

The present work keeps the published RH-to-natural-approximation implication
as an explicit hypothesis until a checked analytic proof is available.
No project-local axiom, `sorry`, or assumed RH-to-`closedSpace` conclusion is
introduced as a theorem. Proving that classical implication conditional on
RH would complete the equivalence; it would not prove RH unconditionally.
