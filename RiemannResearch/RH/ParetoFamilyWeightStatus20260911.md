## Exact mathematical statements

For positive seed sets A, every f in the finite legal span has d > 0 such that every multiple Q of d is a period and, for n < Q,

    f(n) + f(Q - 1 - n) = f(Q - 1).

Consequently rho_3 belongs to the two-seed span but rho_3^2 does not. More strongly, for legal real f,

    f^2 is legal  iff  there exists c such that every f(n) belongs to {0,c}.

Every finite legal f has a positive integer zero; the positive-index constant is excluded independently of the convention at index zero.

The finite nonunital generated algebra contains

    A_K(n) = 1 - product_{j<K}(1-b_j(n)) = 1 - indicator(2^K divides n),

with exact full Pareto squared error W_{2^K}(2^K) <= pi^2/(6*4^K), tending to zero. For K >= 2 these A_K are not legal linear combinations.

The weight convention is

    W_Q(r) = sum_{j>=0} 1/((r+Q*j)(r+Q*j+1)),   1 <= r <= Q.

The representative r=Q is the zero residue class. Lean uses `progressionWeight`; index zero has zero mass. For these representatives, with psi(x)=Re(Complex.digamma(x)),

    W_Q(r) = [psi((r+1)/Q)-psi(r/Q)]/Q

and the first atom can be removed exactly:

    W_Q(r) = 1/[r(r+1)] + [psi(1+(r+1)/Q)-psi(1+r/Q)]/Q.

The function psi is proved strictly increasing and strictly concave throughout the positive real axis. In the midpoint formula m=1+(r+1/2)/Q, the derivative is the actual Lean `deriv` of psi, not a placeholder function:

    W_Q(r) = positiveWeight(r) + psi'(m)/Q^2 + E_Q(r),
    0 < E_Q(r) <= pi^4/(360*Q^4).

The uniform tail bound is

    0 < W_Q(r)-positiveWeight(r) <= pi^2/(6*Q^2).

Writing Z_p=sum_{j>=0}1/(j+1)^p, the complete signed fourth-order expansion is

    W_Q(r) = positiveWeight(r) + pi^2/(6*Q^2)
             - (2r+1)*Z_3/Q^3 + (3r^2+3r+1)*pi^4/(90*Q^4) - S_Q(r),
    0 < S_Q(r) <= (4r^3+6r^2+4r+1)*Z_5/Q^5.

The r-dependent remainder constant is explicit. The fixed-r asymptotic must not be substituted for a uniform approximation on a growing residue chart. `positivePSeries` is the concrete convergent p-series definition of the integer zeta constants; identification with Mathlib's separately defined complex riemannZeta is not needed or asserted.

Both integrals are verified on the open unit interval (equivalently the usual integral from 0 to 1):

    W_Q(r) = integral x^(r-1)*(1-x)/(1-x^Q) dx
           = integral x^(r-1)/(sum_{j<Q}x^j) dx.
