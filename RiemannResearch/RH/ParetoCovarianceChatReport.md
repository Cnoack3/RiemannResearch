# Pareto covariance repairs: infinite projections, composite completion, and forced two-seed cancellation

## 1. What has advanced

The strongest additional conclusion concerns the actual two-seed strategy. Let \(M_2\) and \(M_3\) be the separately closed spaces generated by all integer dilations of the binary and ternary residue seeds. Then

\[
\boxed{1\notin M_2+M_3.}
\]

This excludes an exact decomposition into two finite-energy closed channels. It does **not** exclude approximation by their joint span. More quantitatively, for all sufficiently small positive errors

\[
\varepsilon=\|1-A-B\|,\qquad A\in M_2,\ B\in M_3,
\]

there are fixed positive constants such that

\[
\min(\|A\|,\|B\|)\ge\frac c\varepsilon,
\qquad
\min(\operatorname{Var}A,\operatorname{Var}B)\ge\frac{c'}{\varepsilon^2}.
\]

The full variance can nevertheless tend to zero: its negative cross-covariance must cancel the growing channel variances. In particular,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A,B)\longrightarrow-\infty,
\qquad
0\le1+\operatorname{Corr}(A,B)=O(\varepsilon^4).
\]

Thus a successful two-seed construction must be analyzed through its **joint signed quadratic form**. Requiring bounded energy in the separate channels would exclude every successful approximation.

Several other obligations can now be resolved on paper:

| Question left by the earlier report | Result of this continuation |
|---|---|
| Do infinite reverse pivots exist, and can a residual tail subspace survive? | The pivots converge strongly, form a complete orthogonal basis of the closed triangular span, and the intersection of tail spaces is zero. |

The new obstruction and the finite completion result fit together: arithmetic feasibility is now explicit, while the required infinite covariance cancellation remains the difficult step. RH is unresolved.

## 2. Common normalization, complete tails, and one sign correction

Throughout,

\[
w_k=\frac1{k(k+1)},\qquad
\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_{\ge1},w),\qquad
\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{k\ge1}w_k f(k)\overline{g(k)}.
\]

The inner product is linear in the first argument. Except for the explicitly complex dual-space arguments in Section 3, all approximants and coefficients below are real. Taking real parts cannot increase error against the real target. Residue moduli and dilation indices are positive integers; every seed has modulus at least two. All arithmetic sequences have the auxiliary value \(f(0)=0\); the target \(1\) means one at every positive coordinate and zero at the auxiliary coordinate. Define

\[
m(f)=\langle f,1\rangle,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(f)=\|f\|^2-|m(f)|^2,
\qquad R_b(k)=k\bmod b,
\]

\[
D_nf(k)=nf(\lfloor k/n\rfloor),\qquad
A_n^{(b)}=D_nR_b=R_{bn}-R_n.
\]

Telescoping the weights in blocks of length \(n\) gives

\[
m(D_nf)=m(f),\qquad
\|D_nf\|^2=n\|f\|^2.
\tag{F1}
\]

The mean of a residue is \(m(R_b)=\log b\). One direct proof integrates \(R_b(\lfloor x\rfloor)x^{-2}\) to an integer cutoff and uses \(R_b(\lfloor x\rfloor)=\lfloor x\rfloor-b\lfloor x/b\rfloor\); the surviving harmonic-number difference tends to \(\log b\). Consequently \(m(A_n^{(b)})=\log b\).

The basic binary repair is

\[
U(p,E)=\frac{A_E^{(2)}-A_p^{(2)}}p
=\frac{R_p-R_{2p}-R_E+R_{2E}}p.
\tag{F2}
\]

It is mean zero. It vanishes below \(p\) and has value \(-1\) at \(p\), when \(p<E\).

For the Möbius defect functional

\[
\mathcal L_d(f)=\sum_{r\mid d}\mu(d/r)\bigl(f(r)-f(r-1)\bigr),
\]

one has \(\mathcal L_d(R_n)=-n\mathbf1_{d=n}\) for \(d>1\), and \(\mathcal L_d(1)=\mu(d)\). For distinct conductors \(p,2p,E,2E>1\), the repair therefore has

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_p(U)=-1,\quad \mathcal L_{2p}(U)=2,\quad
\mathcal L_E(U)=E/p,\quad \mathcal L_{2E}(U)=-2E/p.}
\tag{F3}
\]

**Correction to the source noise-to-signal report:** the signs at \(E\) and \(2E\) were printed in the opposite order. Formula (F3) is the corrected identity. The earlier \(2p\) obstruction and variance calculations are unaffected.

For later computations, if \(f,g\) have common integer period \(Q\), their complete infinite Gram entry is the finite expression

\[
\langle f,g\rangle
=\sum_{r=1}^Q f(r)\overline{g(r)}\,\omega_Q(r),\qquad
\omega_Q(r)=\frac1Q\left[\psi\left(\frac{r+1}Q\right)-\psi\left(\frac rQ\right)\right].
\tag{F4}
\]

Indeed \(\omega_Q(r)=\sum_{j\ge0}w_{r+jQ}\), by the digamma difference series. This is a summation of the entire Pareto tail, not a cutoff approximation. We will use \(Q=\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)\) for residue Gram entries.

### A quantitative form of the surviving binary composite obstruction

For a prime \(p\ge7\), the continuous finite-coordinate functional

\[
\Phi_p=\mathcal L_{2p}+2\mathcal L_p
\]

acts by

\[
\Phi_p(f)=f(2p)-f(2p-1)+f(p)-f(p-1)-f(2).
\]

Its squared dual norm is exactly \(10p^2+6\), and \(\Phi_p(1)=-1\). It annihilates every original repair \(U(q,E_q)\), where \(E_q\) is \(3,5\)-smooth and \(q\ge7\) is prime. If a fixed finite residue base \(F_0\) has no conductor \(p\) or \(2p\), then \(\Phi_p(F_0)=0\) too. Hence every \(F\) in that base plus the closed original repair span satisfies

\[
\boxed{\|1-F\|^2\ge\frac1{10p^2+6}.}
\tag{F5}
\]

Such a prime can always be chosen beyond the fixed base's finite conductor support. This gives an explicit positive floor and shows why a change of basis within the old repair span cannot by itself complete the approximation.

## 3. Two closed seed channels: an unconditional quantitative obstruction

This section treats any multiplicatively independent integers \(a,b\ge2\), including \(2,3\). Write

\[
M_b=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{D_nR_b:n\ge1\}^{\,\mathcal H}.
\]

The previously developed *RH Two-Seed Threshold*, version 2, identifies RH with density of the joint two-seed span. That prior qualitative criterion is used only when interpreting the final limiting problem. The obstructions proved here do not assume RH or that criterion.

### 3.1 Exact discrete Mellin dual vectors

Embed \(\mathcal H\) isometrically into \(L^2([1,\infty),x^{-2}dx)\) by extending each sequence constantly across \([k,k+1)\). For \(\Re s>1/2\), put

\[
\mathcal M_s(f)=\int_1^\infty f(\lfloor x\rfloor)x^{-s-1}\,dx.
\]

It is continuous, with exact discrete norm

\[
\|\mathcal M_s\|^2
=\sum_{k\ge1}k(k+1)
\left|\frac{k^{-s}-(k+1)^{-s}}s\right|^2
\le\frac1{2\Re s-1}.
\tag{D1}
\]

Dilation and the residue identity give

\[
\mathcal M_s(D_nf)=n^{1-s}\mathcal M_s(f),\qquad
\mathcal M_s(R_b)=\frac{\zeta(s)(1-b^{1-s})}{s}.
\tag{D2}
\]

For the second formula first take \(\Re s>1\), where termwise integration is absolute, and continue analytically to \(\Re s>0\); the apparent singularity at \(s=1\) is removable and has value \(\log b\).

At \(s=1+it\), the Riesz vector is

\[
h_t(k)=\frac1{w_k}\int_k^{k+1}x^{-2+it}\,dx
=\frac{k^{-1+it}-(k+1)^{-1+it}}{(1-it)w_k}.
\tag{D3}
\]

Thus

\[
\langle f,h_t\rangle=\mathcal M_{1+it}(f),\qquad
\langle1,h_t\rangle=\frac1{1+it},\qquad
t=\frac{2\pi m}{\log b},\ m\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}
\Longrightarrow h_t\perp M_b.
\tag{D4}
\]

These annihilators come from the seed factor, with no assumption on nontrivial zeta zeros.

Define

\[
\mathcal D(t)=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}
\left[1-\cos\left(t\log(1+1/k)\right)\right].
\]

Directly expanding the numerator of (D3) gives

\[
\boxed{\|h_t\|^2=\frac{\mathcal D(t)}{1+t^2},\qquad
\mathcal D(t)\le1+4|t|.}
\tag{D5}
\]

For the upper bound, use \(2(1-\cos u)\le\min(4,u^2)\), \(\log(1+1/k)\le1/k\), and compare the decreasing summand with \(\int_0^\infty\min(4,t^2/x^2)dx=4|t|\).

The leading constant can also be evaluated exactly:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal D(t)\sim\pi|t|,\qquad \|h_t\|^2\sim\pi/|t|.}
\tag{D6}
\]

For \(t>0\), scale \(k=tx\). On \(\delta\le x\le M\) the summands converge uniformly to \(2[1-\cos(1/x)]\); the omitted low and high ranges contribute at most \(4\delta\) and \(O(1/M)\) after division by \(t\). Therefore the limit is

\[
2\int_0^\infty[1-\cos(1/x)]dx
=2\int_0^\infty\frac{1-\cos y}{y^2}dy=\pi.
\]

Specifically, \(\int_0^\infty(1-\cos y)y^{-2}dy=\pi/2\). This follows from \(y^{-2}=\int_0^\infty u e^{-uy}du\) and Tonelli: the integral equals \(\int_0^\infty(1+u^2)^{-1}du\).

### 3.2 Phase alignment gives a stronger derivative estimate

The map \(t\mapsto h_t\) is continuously differentiable in \(\mathcal H\): it is the conditional expectation of \(x^{it}\), and \(\log x\in L^2(x^{-2}dx)\). Set

\[
q_t=e^{-i(t\log t-t)}h_t\qquad(t>0).
\]

The scalar phase preserves the annihilator, norm, and target-pairing modulus. Its derivative satisfies

\[
\boxed{\|q'_t\|^2\le82/t\quad(t\ge1).}
\tag{D7}
\]

Here are the details of the estimate, since this is what removes a logarithmic loss. For \(k\le N=\lfloor t\rfloor\), differentiating (D3) gives

\[
|h'_t(k)-i(\log t)h_t(k)|
\le\frac{2(k+1)}t\,[\log(t/k)+2].
\]

The last cell is covered by \(|\log((k+1)/t)|\le\log(t/k)+1/k\). Consequently its squared weighted sum over the head is at most

\[
\frac8{t^2}\sum_{k\le t}[\log(t/k)+2]^2
\le\frac8{t^2}\int_0^t[\log(t/x)+2]^2dx=\frac{80}t.
\]

Conditional-expectation contraction on the entire remaining tail bounds it by

\[
\int_{N+1}^\infty\frac{\log^2(x/t)}{x^2}dx
\le\int_t^\infty\frac{\log^2(x/t)}{x^2}dx=\frac2t.
\]

Adding these complete contributions proves (D7). In particular,

\[
t\ge2,\quad |u-t|\le1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\|q_t-q_u\|\le\sqrt{164}\,\frac{|t-u|}{\sqrt t}.
\tag{D8}
\]

### 3.3 The uniform reciprocal-error theorem

**Theorem.** There exist \(c_{a,b}>0\) and \(\varepsilon_0>0\) such that

\[
0<\varepsilon=\|1-A-B\|<\varepsilon_0,\quad
A\in M_a,\ B\in M_b
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\min(\|A\|,\|B\|)\ge c_{a,b}/\varepsilon.}
\tag{D9}
\]

Also \(1\notin M_a+M_b\).

**Proof.** Let

\[
\alpha=\frac{\log b}{\log a},\qquad
c_a=\frac{2\pi}{\log a},\qquad c_b=\frac{2\pi}{\log b}.
\]

For every positive integer \(Q\), Dirichlet's pigeonhole approximation supplies \(1\le m\le Q\) and an integer \(\ell\) with \(|m\alpha-\ell|\le1/Q\). This follows by putting the \(Q+1\) fractional parts of \(j\alpha\), \(0\le j\le Q\), into \(Q\) intervals. Since \(\alpha\) is irrational, any such choices have \(m\to\infty\) as \(Q\to\infty\): finitely many nonzero denominators have a positive minimum distance from an integer.

Put \(t=c_am\), \(u=c_b\ell\). For all sufficiently large \(Q\),

\[
2\le t\le c_aQ,\quad |u-t|\le c_b/Q\le1,\quad 0<u\le2t,
\]

and \(q_t\perp M_a\), \(q_u\perp M_b\). Estimate (D8) gives

\[
\|q_t-q_u\|\le\frac{\sqrt{164}\,c_b}{Q\sqrt t}.
\tag{D10}
\]

For \(e=1-A-B\), the exact two identities are

\[
\langle1,q_t\rangle=\langle e,q_t\rangle+\langle B,q_t-q_u\rangle,
\]

\[
\langle1,q_u\rangle=\langle e,q_u\rangle+\langle A,q_u-q_t\rangle.
\tag{D11}
\]

Choose \(Q=\lfloor1/(128c_a\varepsilon^2)\rfloor\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), this is at least \(1/(256c_a\varepsilon^2)\) and

\[
\varepsilon^2\mathcal D(t),\ \varepsilon^2\mathcal D(u)
\le\varepsilon^2(1+8c_aQ)<1/4.
\]

Thus the error term in each identity (D11) consumes at most half the target-pairing modulus. Using \(u\le2t\), \(t\ge1\), and (D10),

\[
\min(\|A\|,\|B\|)
\ge\frac{Q}{2\sqrt{820}\,c_b\sqrt t}
\ge\frac1{32\sqrt{820}\,c_ac_b\varepsilon}
\ge\frac1{1024c_ac_b\varepsilon}.
\]

So \(c_{a,b}=1/(1024c_ac_b)\) is one conservative valid constant, with an unspecified sufficiently small \(\varepsilon_0\). No claim of optimal constants is made.

If \(e=0\), let \(Q\to\infty\) in (D11) without the error term. The same argument forces the fixed channel norms to be at least a constant times \(\sqrt Q\), an impossibility. This proves nonmembership as well. ∎

This proof is uniform over sufficiently small errors; it does not assume a bound on gaps between continued-fraction denominators. Multiplicatively dependent seeds have a common nonzero exact Mellin resonance and already exclude \(1\) by a single annihilator.

### 3.4 Variance growth and the required rate of correlation

This consequence can be proved without importing the previous optimized phase estimates. Set

\[
\delta_b=\frac1{\mathcal D(2\pi/\log b)}>0.
\]

The annihilator (D4) gives \(\operatorname{dist}(1,M_b)^2\ge\delta_b\). For any nonzero \(f\in M_b\), minimize \(\|1-cf\|^2\) over scalars \(c\). It follows that

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(f)\ge\delta_b\|f\|^2.}
\tag{D12}
\]

For the real channels in (D9), put
\(c_*=c_{a,b}^2\min(\delta_a,\delta_b)>0\). Then

\[
V_A,V_B\ge c_*/\varepsilon^2,\qquad
V_{A+B}=\operatorname{Var}(e)\le\varepsilon^2,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(A,B)=\frac{V_{A+B}-V_A-V_B}{2}
\le-\frac{c_*}{\varepsilon^2}+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}.}
\tag{D13}
\]

Let \(\sigma_A=\sqrt{V_A}\), \(\sigma_B=\sqrt{V_B}\). The reverse triangle inequality for the centered channels gives

\[
|\sigma_A-\sigma_B|\le\varepsilon,
\]

and the exact correlation identity is

\[
2\sigma_A\sigma_B(1+\rho)
=V_{A+B}-(\sigma_A-\sigma_B)^2.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{0\le1+\rho\le\frac{\varepsilon^4}{2c_*},\qquad
\frac{V_{A+B}}{V_A+V_B}\le\frac{\varepsilon^4}{2c_*}.}
\tag{D14}
\]

In particular the standard deviations become equal to within \(\varepsilon\), their ratio tends to one, and the cross term cancels an increasing fraction of the diagonal energy. These are necessary conditions for success, not a construction of successful coefficients. If the total mean is exactly one, the squared approximation error itself is the total variance and the noise-to-signal ratio.

### 3.5 Consequences for closure and the next construction

The exact mathematical distinction is

\[
1\notin M_a+M_b,
\qquad
1\in\overline{M_a+M_b}\ \text{is the prior two-seed RH target}.
\]

Under that prior criterion and RH, the algebraic sum of the two closed modules is dense and proper, hence not closed. The variance estimates above are unconditional implications about any sufficiently accurate approximants; they do not establish that those approximants exist.

The finite optimization later in this report must therefore measure the energy of the **combined function**, retaining the full cross block of the seed Gram matrix. The theorem rules out adding separate bounded-channel estimates as an intermediate requirement. It does not settle what happens with three separately closed independent seed channels: the two-frequency approximation argument alone gives no such conclusion.


## 4. Infinite reverse projections and the exact convergence criterion

In this section, \(H\) denotes the Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\).

### 4.1 Infinite reverse orthogonalization exists and is complete

Let \(k_1<k_2<\cdots\) be positive integer anchors and let \(f_i\in H\) satisfy

\[
f_i(k)=0\quad(k<k_i),\qquad f_i(k_i)\ne0.
\]

Put

\[
M_i=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{f_j:j\ge i\},\qquad
v_i=f_i-P_{M_{i+1}}f_i,\qquad h_i=\|v_i\|^2.
\]

Every coordinate evaluation is continuous because
\(|f(k)|\le\|f\|/\sqrt{w_k}\). Hence every vector in \(M_{i+1}\) vanishes below \(k_{i+1}\). Consequently \(v_i\) agrees with \(f_i\) at all coordinates below \(k_{i+1}\), and

\[
h_i\ge w_{k_i}|f_i(k_i)|^2>0.
\]

Moreover,

\[
M_i=\mathbb Rv_i\mathbin{\overset\perp\oplus}M_{i+1}.
\tag{T1.1}
\]

Indeed \(f_i=v_i+P_{M_{i+1}}f_i\), and a finite-dimensional orthogonal extension of a closed space is closed. The \(v_i\) are pairwise orthogonal.

The decreasing tail intersection is automatically zero:

\[
\boxed{\bigcap_iM_i=\{0\}.}
\tag{T1.2}
\]

For each fixed coordinate \(k\), membership in sufficiently remote \(M_i\) forces its value to vanish. Therefore the vectors \(v_i/\sqrt{h_i}\) form a complete orthonormal basis of \(M_1\). Explicitly, for every \(x\in M_1\),

\[
x=\sum_{i\ge1}\frac{\langle x,v_i\rangle}{h_i}v_i,
\qquad
\|x\|^2=\sum_{i\ge1}\frac{|\langle x,v_i\rangle|^2}{h_i}.
\tag{T1.3}
\]

The projections \(P_{M_i}x\) tend strongly to zero. One can prove this without invoking an additional compactness theorem: for \(j>i\),

\[
\|P_{M_i}x-P_{M_j}x\|^2
=\|P_{M_i}x\|^2-\|P_{M_j}x\|^2.
\]

Thus they are Cauchy, and their limit lies in every \(M_i\), hence vanishes by (T1.2).

#### Passage from finite pivots

For \(N\ge i\), define

\[
v_i^{(N)}=f_i-P_{\operatorname{span}\{f_{i+1},\ldots,f_N\}}f_i.
\]

Increasing-subspace projection convergence gives \(v_i^{(N)}\to v_i\) strongly. More precisely,

\[
\boxed{\|v_i^{(N)}-v_i\|^2
=\|v_i^{(N)}\|^2-h_i\downarrow0.}
\tag{T1.4}
\]

Thus the infinite basis and its positive pivots exist unconditionally for the actual triangular prime repairs. An infinite pivot belongs to the **closed** repair span; it need not itself be a finite legal packet sum. Its finite-pivot approximants are legal finite sums.

### 4.2 Exact transformed coefficients and the escaping boundary

Existence of the infinite basis does not imply convergence of the original prescribed series. The missing condition has an exact form.

Write

\[
b_{ji}=\frac{\langle f_j,v_i\rangle}{h_i}.
\]

Then \(b_{ji}=0\) for \(i<j\), \(b_{jj}=1\), and
\(f_j=\sum_{i\ge j}b_{ji}v_i\) in \(H\). For a prescribed scalar sequence \(a_j\), put

\[
F_N=\sum_{j\le N}a_jf_j,\qquad
\beta_i=\sum_{j\le i}a_jb_{ji}.
\]

The coefficients \(\beta_i\) retain all contributions from earlier generators. Orthogonal decomposition gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
F_N=\sum_{i\le N}\beta_iv_i+B_N,
\qquad B_N=P_{M_{N+1}}F_N.
}
\tag{T2.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\|F_N\|^2=\sum_{i\le N}h_i\beta_i^2+\|B_N\|^2.
}
\tag{T2.2}
\]

The boundary has a full coefficient formula:

\[
\|B_N\|^2
=\sum_{i>N}h_i
\left|\sum_{j\le N}a_jb_{ji}\right|^2.
\tag{T2.3}
\]

**Convergence criterion.** The original partial sums \(F_N\) converge strongly if and only if

\[
\sum_i h_i\beta_i^2<\infty
\quad\text{and}\quad
\|B_N\|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{T2.4}
\]

If these hold, their limit is \(\sum_i\beta_iv_i\). Conversely, if \(F_N\to x\), continuity of the fixed projections gives \(\langle x,v_i\rangle=\beta_ih_i\), while

\[
\|B_N\|\le\|F_N-x\|+\|P_{M_{N+1}}x\|\to0.
\]

This is the required distinction between a zero *intersection* of fixed tail spaces and a moving boundary \(P_{M_{N+1}}F_N\): the former is automatic; the latter need not vanish.

#### A bounded, mean-zero counterexample to naive triangular summation

Let \(\delta_n\) denote the indicator of coordinate \(n\), and take

\[
f_n=\frac{\delta_n}{w_n}-\frac{\delta_{n+1}}{w_{n+1}}.
\]

These are triangular, finite-support, mean-zero vectors. Their closed span is \(1^\perp\): if \(y\) is orthogonal to all of them, then \(y(n)-y(n+1)=0\), so \(y\) is constant.

The bounded sequence \(x=1-2\delta_1\) has mean zero and belongs to this closed span. Its unique coordinatewise triangular expansion uses

\[
a_n=\sum_{k\le n}w_kx(k)=-\frac1{n+1}.
\]

The partial sum equals \(x\) through coordinate \(N\), equals \(N+2\) at \(N+1\), and is zero later. Therefore

\[
\left\|\sum_{n\le N}a_nf_n-x\right\|^2
=\frac{N+1}{N+2}+\frac1{N+2}=1.
\tag{T2.5}
\]

Thus even membership of a bounded mean-zero target in the closed span, unique exact coordinate repair, and a complete reverse orthogonal basis do not justify the raw ordered sum.

### 4.3 The sharp head-and-mean floor

Let a vector \(f\in H\) have prescribed head values \(r_k=f(k)\) for \(k\le N\), and prescribed total mean \(m\). Set

\[
A=\sum_{k\le N}w_kr_k,\qquad
q_N=\sum_{k>N}w_k=\frac1{N+1}.
\]

The exact minimum squared norm among all such vectors is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\le N}w_kr_k^2+(N+1)(m-A)^2.
}
\tag{T3.1}
\]

The unique minimizer has constant tail \((N+1)(m-A)\). For any admissible \(f\), the complete identity is

\[
\|f\|^2
=\sum_{k\le N}w_kr_k^2+(N+1)(m-A)^2
+\sum_{k>N}w_k\left[f(k)-(N+1)(m-A)\right]^2.
\tag{T3.2}
\]

The cross term vanishes because the last tail remainder has mean zero.

For a mean-zero repair with values zero below \(p\) and \(-1\) at \(p\), (T3.1) sharpens the earlier report's anchor estimate to

\[
\boxed{\|f\|^2\ge\frac1{p^2}.}
\tag{T3.3}
\]

The universal minimizer is \(-1\) at \(p\), \(1/p\) above \(p\), and zero earlier. This minimizer need not lie in the legal repair closure.

More generally, if \(f=-1\) on \([p,q)\cap\mathbb N\), is zero below \(p\), and has mean zero, then

\[
\boxed{\|f\|^2\ge\frac{q-p}{p^2}.}
\tag{T3.4}
\]

The unique universal minimizer is

\[
g_{p,q}(k)=-1_{p\le k<q}+\frac{q-p}{p}1_{k\ge q}.
\tag{T3.5}
\]

Its norm is exactly \((q-p)/p^2\). For successive disjoint intervals these \(g_{p,q}\) are mutually orthogonal: the earlier vector is constant on the support of the later mean-zero vector.

#### Exact arithmetic excess for tail optimization

Let \(A_N\) be the closed space of mean-zero vectors vanishing through \(N\), and let \(T\subseteq A_N\) be the closed legal tail space. Let \(u\) be the universal head-and-mean minimizer from (T3.1). Then

\[
f-P_Tf=u+(I-P_T)(f-u),\qquad
\|f-P_Tf\|^2=\|u\|^2+\|(I-P_T)(f-u)\|^2.
\tag{T3.6}
\]

The second term is exactly the additional energy caused by the restrictions on legal tail directions. This identifies a concrete arithmetic quantity to estimate beyond the unavoidable head-and-mean cost.



## 5. Elementary summable repairs within the original prime span

Use the original least-smooth scale \(E_p\), the smallest \(3^a5^b>p\), for primes \(p\ge7\). Set

\[
U_p=U(p,E_p),\qquad
U(p,E)=\frac{R_p-R_{2p}-R_E+R_{2E}}p.
\]

All these vectors are mean-zero legal mixed-packet sums. Elementary comparison with a power of \(3\) gives

\[
p<E_p<3p.
\tag{T4.1}
\]

Partition the primes into finite nonempty clusters having the same \(E_p=E\). For an interior prime \(p\) in its cluster, let \(q\) be the next prime in that cluster. Define

\[
W_p=U_p-\frac qpU_q=U(p,q).
\tag{T4.2}
\]

For the last prime \(p\) in the cluster, set \(W_p=U_p\). This is a finite invertible upper triangular change of generators on each cluster. Thus

\[
\operatorname{span}\{W_p:p\ge7\}
=\operatorname{span}\{U_p:p\ge7\}.
\tag{T4.3}
\]

In particular every existing annihilating composite functional survives. The new generators transport defects to the next prime; they do not independently preserve every other prime defect.

### The coefficient transport is explicit

In one cluster write \(p_1<\cdots<p_m\) and \(U_i=U_{p_i}\). Then

\[
U_i=\sum_{j=i}^m\frac{p_j}{p_i}W_{p_j}.
\]

Consequently, for arbitrary real coefficients in a complete finite cluster,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^ma_iU_i
=\sum_{j=1}^m\alpha_jW_{p_j},\qquad
\alpha_j=p_j\sum_{i\le j}\frac{a_i}{p_i}.
}
\tag{T4.3a}
\]

The final anchor has coefficient \(p_m\sum_i a_i/p_i\). It disappears exactly when the reciprocal-weighted sum vanishes. For \(a_i=1\), the coefficient is the cumulative reciprocal sum multiplied by \(p_j\), and can grow with cluster size. The energy estimate below provides no bound on these coefficients.

For an interior prime the only changed prime defects are

\[
\mathcal L_p(W_p)=-1,\qquad
\mathcal L_q(W_p)=\frac qp;
\]

all other prime defects vanish. The accompanying composite defects are \(\mathcal L_{2p}(W_p)=2\) and \(\mathcal L_{2q}(W_p)=-2q/p\). For a terminal repair \(U(p,E)\), its four nonzero conductor defects are

\[
\mathcal L_p=-1,\quad \mathcal L_{2p}=2,\quad
\mathcal L_E=E/p,\quad\mathcal L_{2E}=-2E/p.
\]

These signs agree with the corrected identity (F3).

### The elementary energy bound

The exact square-wave expression and its nonnegative cross product give, for \(a\le b\),

\[
\|U(a,b)\|^2\le\frac{a+b}{a^2}\log2.
\tag{T4.4}
\]

The following elementary short-gap estimate holds when \(h=b-a\le a\):

\[
\|U(a,b)\|^2
\le8\frac h{a^2}\left(1+\log\frac ah\right).
\tag{T4.5}
\]

Here is a self-contained proof of the estimate used. Write \(b_0(x)=\lfloor x\rfloor\bmod2\), \(r=1+\delta=b/a\), and use the exact Pareto integral

\[
\|U(a,b)\|^2=\frac1a\Gamma(r),\qquad
\Gamma(r)=\int_0^\infty[rb_0(x/r)-b_0(x)]^2\frac{dx}{x^2}.
\]

For \(0<\delta\le1/2\), partition at \(x=1/\delta\). In the head, wherever the two waves agree the contribution is at most \(\delta^2\). Where they disagree they lie in the union of the intervals \([j,rj)\), \(1\le j\le\lfloor1/\delta\rfloor\); each has exact mass \(\delta/(rj)\), and the integrand is at most \(r^2\). The entire remaining tail costs at most \(r^2\delta\). Thus, keeping all three contributions bounded explicitly,

\[
\Gamma(r)\le\delta^2+r\delta H_{\lfloor1/\delta\rfloor}+r^2\delta
\le\tfrac{17}4\delta+\tfrac32\delta\log(1/\delta)
\le8\delta(1+\log(1/\delta)).
\]

The harmonic bound follows by integral comparison. For \(1/2<\delta\le1\), the exact nonnegative cross product in (T4.4) gives \(\Gamma(r)\le(1+r)\log2\le3\log2<4\le8\delta(1+\log(1/\delta))\). This proves (T4.5) in its entire stated range.

For interior primes with \(p\ge P\ge7\) and \(h=q-p\le p\), we have \(h\ge1\), so

\[
\|W_p\|^2
\le8\frac h{p^2}(1+\log p)
\le32\int_p^q\frac{1+\log x}{x^2}\,dx.
\]

The intervals \([p,q]\) have disjoint interiors. Consequently their total energy is at most

\[
32\frac{\log P+2}{P}.
\tag{T4.6}
\]

For interior primes with \(h>p\), (T4.1) implies \(q<E_p<3p\), and hence

\[
\|W_p\|^2\le\frac{4\log2}{p}
\le8\log2\int_p^q\frac{dx}{x^2}.
\]

Their total is at most \(8\log2/P\). This split avoids requiring even a bound for consecutive prime gaps.

For each terminal prime, (T4.4) and (T4.1) give

\[
\|W_p\|^2\le\frac{4\log2}{p}
\le\frac{12\log2}{E_p}.
\]

There is at most one terminal prime per smooth number \(E\). Direct geometric summation proves

\[
\sum_{\substack{a,b\ge0\\3^a5^b\ge P}}\frac1{3^a5^b}
\le\frac1P\left[\frac54\left(1+\frac{\log P}{\log3}\right)+\frac{15}8\right].
\tag{T4.7}
\]

For completeness, separate \(3^a<P\) and \(3^a\ge P\). In the first range, summing \(5^{-b}\) above its threshold and multiplying by \(3^{-a}\) costs at most \(5/(4P)\) for each \(a\); there are at most \(1+\log P/\log3\) such indices. In the second range the full double geometric tail costs at most \(15/(8P)\).

Combining these estimates proves the unconditional explicit bound

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ge P}\|W_p\|^2
\le\frac{32(\log P+2)+8\log2}{P}
+\frac{12\log2}{P}
\left[\frac54\left(1+\frac{\log P}{\log3}\right)+\frac{15}8\right].
}
\tag{T4.8}
\]

No quantitative smooth-number spacing theorem, prime-counting estimate, RH assumption, or asymptotic cancellation hypothesis appears in this argument. The constants are intentionally conservative.

### Consequences for the infinite pivots

Order all original primes increasingly and form the infinite reverse pivots \(v_p\) of Section 4.1 from \(U_p\). For interior primes, \(W_p\) differs from \(U_p\) by a future generator; for terminal primes they are identical. Therefore

\[
\|v_p\|^2\le\|W_p\|^2,
\qquad
\boxed{\sum_{p\ge P}\|v_p\|^2\le\text{the right side of (4.8)}.}
\tag{T4.9}
\]

For an interior prime with \(q\le2p\), the original \(U_p\), and hence its reverse pivot, equals \(-1\) on \([p,q)\). The complete two-sided estimate is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{q-p}{p^2}
\le\|v_p\|^2
\le8\frac{q-p}{p^2}\left(1+\log\frac p{q-p}\right).
}
\tag{T4.10}
\]

More precisely,

\[
\|v_p\|^2=\frac{q-p}{p^2}+\|v_p-g_{p,q}\|^2,
\tag{T4.11}
\]

with the second term measuring the unavailable legal tail correction. This is a concrete comparison target for the arithmetic projection problem.




### What diagonal summability now does justify

For every finitely supported coefficient family, first retain the exact Gram equality and then apply the separate Cauchy–Schwarz estimate:

\[
\left\|\sum_p a_pW_p\right\|^2
=\sum_{p,q}a_pa_q\langle W_p,W_q\rangle
\le\left(\sum_p|a_p|^2\right)\left(\sum_p\|W_p\|^2\right).
\]

The new theorem therefore supplies a bounded synthesis map from ordinary \(\ell^2\) coefficients into \(\mathcal H\). It does not give the refuted bound with \(\sum_p|a_p|^2\|U_p\|^2\) on the right, and it does not place the constant coefficient sequence in \(\ell^2\).

For the orthogonal pivots, \(\sum_p\|v_p\|^2<\infty\) does justify \(\sum_pv_p\) and, more generally, bounded coefficients in that basis. Translating a prescribed original repair series into this basis still requires both conditions of (T2.4). All these vectors remain in the old span and retain the positive approximation floor (F5).


## 6. All-integer adjacent repairs: exact covariance and a sharp coefficient threshold

Here \(U_n\) denotes the adjacent repair \(U(n,n+1)\), not the prime family \(U_p=U(p,E_p)\) of Section 5.

### 6.1 Definitions and the change of repair family

Let
\[
w_k=\frac1{k(k+1)},\qquad H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_{\ge1},w),\qquad
b(x)=\lfloor x\rfloor\bmod2,
\]
and define
\[
A_n(k)=n\,b(k/n)=R_{2n}(k)-R_n(k),\quad
U_n=(A_{n+1}-A_n)/n.
\]
Thus
\[
U_n=\frac{R_n-R_{2n}-R_{n+1}+R_{2n+2}}n.
\]
Every $U_n$ is a legal finite residue combination and has mean zero. It vanishes at all coordinates $k<n$ and has value $-1$ at $k=n$. For $n\ge2$, the four nonzero Möbius defects are
\[
\mathcal L_n(U_n)=-1,\quad
\mathcal L_{n+1}(U_n)=\frac{n+1}{n},\quad
\mathcal L_{2n}(U_n)=2,\quad
\mathcal L_{2n+2}(U_n)=-\frac{2(n+1)}n.
\]
Unlike repairs anchored at an odd $3,5$-smooth integer, this enlarged family need not consist solely of mixed packets. Its mean remains exactly zero because the logarithmic means cancel. The adjacent conductors are available when arbitrary integer dilations are allowed.

The short-gap estimate (T4.5) immediately gives
\[
\|U_n\|_H^2\le\frac{8(1+\log n)}{n^2},\qquad
\sum_{n\ge1}\|U_n\|_H^2<\infty.
\]
No smooth-number spacing theorem is needed for this all-integer diagonal summability. What is gained and what is lost are explicit: all earlier coordinates are preserved, but the defect at $n+1$ is changed, and the coefficient direction for a global arithmetic correction remains to be determined.

### 6.2 Full covariance, with three exact representations

Write $K(a,b)=\langle A_a,A_b\rangle_H$. Since the integer-step functions are constant on $[k,k+1)$,
\[
K(a,b)=ab\int_0^\infty b(x/a)b(x/b)\frac{dx}{x^2},
\qquad K(a,a)=a\log2.
\tag{A1}
\]
The adjacent covariance is the exact double difference
\[
\boxed{\langle U_m,U_n\rangle=
\frac{K(m+1,n+1)-K(m+1,n)-K(m,n+1)+K(m,n)}{mn}.}
\tag{A2}
\]
Every mixed term is present. Also $K(ga,gb)=gK(a,b)$ for positive integers $g$.

For an exact finite periodic formula, let $Q=2\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)$ and
\[
\omega_Q(r)=\frac1Q\left[\psi\left(\frac{r+1}Q\right)-\psi\left(\frac rQ\right)\right],\quad1\le r\le Q.
\]
Then
\[
K(a,b)=ab\sum_{r=1}^Q\omega_Q(r)b(r/a)b(r/b).
\tag{A3}
\]
The weights sum the complete infinite Pareto tail in each congruence class.

For an overlap representation, put $L_{rs}=\max((2r+1)a,(2s+1)b)$ and $T_{rs}=\min((2r+2)a,(2s+2)b)$. Then
\[
K(a,b)=ab\sum_{r,s\ge0}\left(\frac1{L_{rs}}-\frac1{T_{rs}}\right)_+.
\tag{A4}
\]
This is a sum of the exact masses of the intersections of the odd square-wave intervals. Nonnegative terms justify the rearrangement.

There is also an exact spectral expression. Let
\[
\eta(s)=\sum_{j\ge1}(-1)^{j-1}j^{-s},\qquad
q(s)=\eta(s)/s,\quad \Re s>0.
\]
Integration over the odd intervals gives
\[
\int_0^\infty b(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx=q(s).
\]
Mellin–Plancherel, equivalently Fourier–Plancherel after $x=e^t$, therefore gives
\[
K(a,b)=\frac{\sqrt{ab}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
|q(1/2+it)|^2(b/a)^{it}\,dt.
\tag{A5}
\]
For any finite real coefficient family $a_n$,
\[
\boxed{
\left\|\sum_n a_nU_n\right\|_H^2=
\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|q(1/2+it)|^2
\left|\sum_n\frac{a_n}{n}
\big((n+1)^{1/2-it}-n^{1/2-it}\big)\right|^2dt.}
\tag{A6}
\]
This retains the arithmetic phases and every covariance. It supplies an exact object for a chosen coefficient direction without asserting phase independence.

### 6.3 A useful coefficient estimate from the positive dilation kernel

The elementary inequality $b(x/a)b(x/b)\le b(x/\max(a,b))$ gives
\[
0\le K(a,b)\le(\log2)\min(a,b).
\tag{A7}
\]
Consequently, for every finitely supported real sequence $c_n$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|\sum_nc_nA_n\right\|_H^2
&=\sum_{m,n}c_mc_nK(m,n)\\
&\le(\log2)\sum_{m,n}|c_mc_n|\min(m,n)\\
&=(\log2)\sum_{j\ge1}\left(\sum_{n\ge j}|c_n|\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A8}
\]
The first line is the full signed equality; the estimate is a separate sufficient bound. No assertion of positive-semidefinite domination of the entire signed kernel is needed.

To apply this, write $t_n=a_n/n$. Exact summation by parts is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{n=M}^N a_nU_n=
-t_MA_M+\sum_{n=M+1}^N(t_{n-1}-t_n)A_n+t_NA_{N+1}.}
\tag{A9}
\]
The final anchor remains visible. For arbitrary coefficients, (A8) applied to these transformed coefficients yields a bound by the squared tails of their absolute variation. This can be much better than bounding the individual repair norms separately.

#### Theorem: monotone reciprocal coefficients

Suppose $t_M\ge t_{M+1}\ge\cdots\ge t_N\ge0$. Then
\[
\boxed{\left\|\sum_{n=M}^N nt_nU_n\right\|_H^2
\le (\log2)\left(2Mt_M^2+\sum_{n=M}^Nt_n^2\right).}
\tag{A10}
\]

**Proof.** Write (A9) as $-t_MA_M+P$ with all coefficients of $P$ nonnegative. Its exact norm is
\[
Mt_M^2\log2+\|P\|^2-2t_M\langle A_M,P\rangle.
\]
The last term has its displayed sign because $A_M,P\ge0$ pointwise. For the positive coefficients of $P$, their cumulative tail is $t_M$ at indices $j\le M+1$, is $t_{j-1}$ for $M+1<j\le N+1$, and is zero thereafter. Equation (A8) therefore bounds $\|P\|^2$ by
\[
(\log2)\left(Mt_M^2+\sum_{n=M}^Nt_n^2\right).
\]
Substitution proves (A10). This proof records the negative cross term exactly before taking the estimate. ∎

#### Corollary: a broad, unconditional convergence class

If $t_n\ge0$ is nonincreasing and $\sum_nt_n^2<\infty$, then
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}nt_nU_n
\]
converges in $H$ in the natural order. Indeed monotonicity and square summability imply $Mt_M^2\to0$, and (A10) is the Cauchy estimate.

Examples include
\[
a_n=1;\qquad a_n=n^\theta\ (\theta<1/2);\qquad
a_n=\frac{\sqrt n}{(\log(en))^\gamma}\ (\gamma>1/2).
\]
The last example is stronger than the sufficient estimate obtained by demanding absolute summability of $|t_{n-1}-t_n|\sqrt n$, which would require $\gamma>1$.

In particular, the coefficient choice $a_n=1$, which was not justified merely by summable diagonal energies, **is justified for the all-integer adjacent family**:
\[
S=\sum_{n\ge1}U_n=-A_1+\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{A_n}{n(n-1)}.
\tag{A11}
\]
For $M\ge2$,
\[
\left\|\sum_{n\ge M}U_n\right\|_H^2
\le(\log2)\left(\frac2M+\sum_{n\ge M}\frac1{n^2}\right)
\le(\log2)\left(\frac2M+\frac1{M-1}\right).
\tag{A12}
\]
The right-hand series in (A11) is even absolutely convergent in $H$, since $\|A_n\|=\sqrt{n\log2}$. Every partial sum and its limit have mean zero. This is convergence to a specific control function, not convergence to the constant target.

### 6.4 Sharpness: an explicit critical coefficient obstruction

The exponent $1/2$ above is sharp for positive power coefficients. Let
\[
S_{\theta,N}=\sum_{n=1}^Nn^\theta U_n,\qquad0<\theta<1.
\]
For every fixed integer $k$, this stabilizes once $N\ge k$, to
\[
S_\theta(k)=-A_1(k)+\sum_{n=2}^k
\big((n-1)^{\theta-1}-n^{\theta-1}\big)A_n(k).
\tag{A13}
\]
The coefficient expansion
\[
n\big((n-1)^{\theta-1}-n^{\theta-1}\big)
=(1-\theta)n^{\theta-1}+O_\theta(n^{\theta-2})
\]
has an absolutely summable error. Hence
\[
S_\theta(k)=(1-\theta)\sum_{n\le k}n^{\theta-1}b(k/n)+O_\theta(1).
\]
The Riemann sums converge after scaling by $k^{-\theta}$:
\[
\frac1k\sum_{n\le k}(n/k)^{\theta-1}b(k/n)
\longrightarrow\int_0^1y^{\theta-1}b(1/y)\,dy
=\frac{\eta(\theta)}\theta.
\]
For completeness, on $[\varepsilon,1]$ the integrand has only finitely many discontinuities. On $(0,\varepsilon)$ both the integral and its positive Riemann sums are $O_\theta(\varepsilon^\theta)$. This proves the limit without an unjustified smoothness assumption near zero. Therefore
\[
\boxed{S_\theta(k)\sim C_\theta k^\theta,
\qquad C_\theta=\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\eta(\theta)>0.}
\tag{A14}
\]

At the boundary $\theta=1/2$, $C_{1/2}=\eta(1/2)$. For $k\le N$, the finite sum equals the stabilized value. For $k>N$, (A9) gives the uniform bound $|S_{1/2,N}(k)|=O(\sqrt N)$, so the entire tail contributes $O(1)$ to the squared norm. Equation (A14) gives
\[
\boxed{\|S_{1/2,N}\|_H^2
=\eta(1/2)^2\log N+o(\log N).}
\tag{A15}
\]
Thus the critical series diverges with an exact leading energy constant. For $1/2<\theta<1$, (A14) gives a lower bound of order $N^{2\theta-1}$, while (A10) gives the matching upper order:
\[
\|S_{\theta,N}\|_H^2\asymp_\theta N^{2\theta-1}.
\tag{A16}
\]
For $\theta<1/2$, convergence follows from (A10). These statements give an actual necessary threshold for this explicit coefficient family, rather than a diagonal-norm heuristic.

The spectral explanation is also exact. In $\Re s>\theta$, the Mellin transform of (A13) is
\[
\frac{\eta(s)}s\sum_{n\ge1}n^{\theta-1}
\big((n+1)^{1-s}-n^{1-s}\big).
\]
The Dirichlet series has a pole at $s=\theta$ with residue $1-\theta$. At $\theta=1/2$, the nonzero factor $\eta(1/2)$ leaves the pole on the norm's Mellin boundary, matching (A15). This interpretation is not needed for the elementary proof.

### 6.5 What this supplies and what it does not

The old shared-smooth-scale family is not repaired by simply keeping its original coefficients. The present result enlarges the scale family, computes its entire covariance exactly, and proves convergence for several specified coefficient directions, including all coefficients equal to one. It removes the smooth-gap input for diagonal summability and shows that collective convergence can also be proved for a substantial class.

The missing arithmetic step is now particularly concrete: derive the coefficients required for simultaneous prime and composite correction and show that their transformed coefficient tails obey a valid bound such as (A8), or exploit their signed kernel directly in (A6). The monotonicity hypothesis of (A10) is not known for those forced coefficients. All constructions here use the binary dilation seed, so any independent one-seed annihilator or two-seed compatibility obstruction still applies. No conclusion about approximation of $1$, the full Weil form, or RH follows from these convergence theorems alone.


## 7. Complete finite composite equations and the exact joint minimum

The head length is now \(h\); \(M\) is the independent dilation budget.

### 7.1 Normalization and a triangular inverse

Put
\[
w_k=\frac1{k(k+1)},\quad \mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_{>0},w),\quad
R_b(k)=k\bmod b,\quad
A_n^{(b)}=D_nR_b=R_{bn}-R_n,
\]
where \(D_nf(k)=nf(\lfloor k/n\rfloor)\), and define
\(h_n^{(b)}=A_n^{(b)}/n\). All functions are extended by zero at zero. For \(d\ge1\), write
\[
\mathcal L_d(f)=\sum_{r\mid d}\mu(d/r)\{f(r)-f(r-1)\}.
\]
The exact calculation
\[
\Delta h_n^{(b)}(k)=\mathbf1_{n\mid k}-b\mathbf1_{bn\mid k}
\]
gives
\[
\mathcal L_d\left(\sum_nx_nh_n^{(b)}\right)
=x_d-b\mathbf1_{b\mid d}x_{d/b}.                     \tag{C1}
\]
This includes \(d=1\). The target has \(\mathcal L_d(1)=\mu(d)\).

Consequently, exact agreement with 1 through coordinate \(h\) forces and is forced by
\[
x_d=\sum_{j\ge0:b^j\mid d}b^j\mu(d/b^j),\qquad d\le h. \tag{C2}
\]
The sum is finite. To prove sufficiency, apply Möbius inversion to (C1): the first difference agrees with that of the target through \(h\), and both functions start at zero.

For prime \(b\), if \(d=b^vm\) with \((b,m)=1\), (C2) becomes
\[
\boxed{x_{b^vm}=\varphi(b^v)\mu(m).}                 \tag{C3}
\]
For \(v=0\) this uses \(\varphi(1)=1\). For \(v\ge1\), only the terms \(j=v,v-1\) survive. Thus one seed can solve every finite prime and composite defect, and even gives a locally finite pointwise expansion of 1. It also forces large coefficients along its prime-power rays. Neither local finiteness nor finite head solvability implies convergence in \(\mathcal H\). The single-seed Mellin annihilators in Section 3.1 give an independent obstruction to that conclusion.

### 7.2 The complete two-seed freedom

For normalized coefficients \(x,y\), let
\[
(T_bx)_d=x_d-b\mathbf1_{b\mid d}x_{d/b}.
\]
The two-seed head equations are exactly
\[
T_2x+T_3y=\mu\quad\text{through }h.                 \tag{C4}
\]
The two operators commute and each is invertible on formal arithmetic sequences, with the locally finite inverse in (C2). If \(a=T_2^{-1}\mu\), **all** solutions through \(h\) are
\[
\boxed{x=a+T_3z,\qquad y=-T_2z\quad(d\le h),}       \tag{C5}
\]
where \(z_1,\ldots,z_h\) are arbitrary. Indeed, for a given solution define \(z=-T_2^{-1}y\); then (C4) gives the asserted formula for \(x\). Conversely, commutation verifies (C4).

The corresponding pointwise generator identity is
\[
h_n^{(2)}-3h_{3n}^{(2)}-h_n^{(3)}+2h_{2n}^{(3)}=0. \tag{C6}
\]
For the unnormalized generators it reads
\[
A_n^{(2)}-A_{3n}^{(2)}-A_n^{(3)}+A_{2n}^{(3)}=0.   \tag{C7}
\]
All four terms are retained. The identities follow immediately by writing each \(A\) as a difference of two residues.

There is also a global finite-support statement. Write a finite combination as
\(F=\sum_nc_nA_n^{(2)}+d_nA_n^{(3)}\), and let
\((S_bc)_n=\mathbf1_{b\mid n}c_{n/b}\). Its full finite kernel is
\[
c=(I-S_3)z,\qquad d=-(I-S_2)z,                    \tag{C8}
\]
with finitely supported \(z\). For a proof, split the indices into orbits \(m2^a3^b\), \((m,6)=1\), and identify the finite arrays with polynomials \(C(u,v),D(u,v)\). The zero condition is
\((1-u)C+(1-v)D=0\). Evaluating at \(u=1\) shows that \(D\) is divisible by \(1-u\), and substitution gives (C8). The implication from a zero function to the coefficient zero condition is justified by residue independence in the next section. Formula (C8) concerns unrestricted finite supports; a cutoff imposes its own support conditions on \(z\).

### 7.3 A finite graph solves every composite equation

Separate the head length \(h\) from the dilation budget \(M\), with \(1\le h\le M\). Let
\[
\mathcal V_M=\operatorname{span}\{A_n^{(2)},A_n^{(3)}:1\le n\le M\},
\qquad
S_M=\{1,\ldots,M\}\cup2\{1,\ldots,M\}\cup3\{1,\ldots,M\}.
\]
Consider the graph on \(S_M\) whose edges are \(n\to2n\) and \(n\to3n\), for \(n\le M\). An edge is precisely the residue difference \(R_{bn}-R_n\).

Define the \(6\)-free core
\[
\operatorname{core}(n)=n/\bigl(2^{v_2(n)}3^{v_3(n)}\bigr).
\]
The connected components of this graph are precisely its fixed-core sets, indexed by \(m\le M\) with \((m,6)=1\). Every interior vertex can be connected to its core by repeatedly dividing by 2 or 3; every outer vertex has an interior parent. This proves the claim.

For a finite residue representation \(F=\sum_{d\in S_M}\beta_dR_d\), keep a *virtual* coefficient \(\beta_1\), although \(R_1=0\). Then
\[
\boxed{F\in\mathcal V_M\ \Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{the coefficients can be chosen with }
\sum_{\operatorname{core}(d)=m}\beta_d=0
\text{ for every component }m.}                  \tag{C9}
\]
Necessity is the incidence identity. Sufficiency follows from a spanning tree in each connected component: any vertex coefficient vector with total zero is the boundary of a signed finite edge flow. Equivalently, fix a root; write the vector as a sum of multiples of differences of a vertex and the root, and telescope each difference along a graph path.

The nontrivial residues \(R_d\), \(d>1\), are linearly independent in every finite family: \(\mathcal L_d(\sum_{e>1}\beta_eR_e)=-d\beta_d\) for \(d>1\). Therefore their coefficients are unique. The virtual coefficient \(\beta_1\) is then also unique in (C9). Because the full coefficient sum is zero,
\(\mathcal L_1(F)=F(1)=-\beta_1\). Thus the uniform formula
\[
\mathcal L_d(F)=-d\beta_d                       \tag{C10}
\]
holds at every \(d\in S_M\), including 1, for these incidence coefficients.

Put \(B=S_M\setminus\{1,\ldots,h\}\). Exact agreement with 1 through \(h\) is now completely characterized by
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\beta_d&=-\mu(d)/d&& (1\le d\le h),\\
\sum_{d\in B:\operatorname{core}(d)=m}\beta_d
&=C_m(h)&& ((m,6)=1,\ m\le M),
\end{aligned}}                                                       \tag{C11}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
C_m(h)=\frac{\mu(m)}m
\left(\mathbf1_{m\le h}-\frac12\mathbf1_{2m\le h}
-\frac13\mathbf1_{3m\le h}+\frac16\mathbf1_{6m\le h}\right).}          \tag{C12}
\]
For \(6m\le h\), the right side is \(\mu(m)/(3m)\); for \(m>h\), it is zero. Formula (C12) follows because all other powers of 2 or 3 have zero Möbius function. It remains true when \(\mu(m)=0\).

**Proof that (C11) is sufficient.** Its second line makes the component totals in (C9) zero after inserting the fixed first line. Hence a legal two-seed representation exists. Formula (C10) gives the complete target defect through \(h\), not merely its prime part. Möbius inversion then gives \(\Delta F(k)=\mathbf1_{k=1}\) for \(k\le h\), hence the required head values. Necessity follows by reversing these steps.

This is an exact solution of the finite composite completion problem. It does not estimate the Hilbert norm of the completed tail.

### 7.4 An explicit completion and exact mean correction

For each core \(m\le h\), choose the least \(a_m=2^{j_m}m>h\). Then \(a_m\le2h\), its predecessor is at most \(h\), and it is a vertex of \(S_h\). Define
\[
F_h=\sum_{n\le h}\frac{\mu(n)}n
\bigl(R_{a_{\operatorname{core}(n)}}-R_n\bigr).       \tag{C13}
\]
Every difference displayed belongs to \(\mathcal V_h\): move from \(n\) down to its core through interior vertices and from that core up the chosen power-of-2 path. Thus (C13) is an explicit finite two-seed construction.

It has the residue form
\[
F_h=-\sum_{2\le n\le h}\frac{\mu(n)}nR_n
+\sum_{\substack{m\le h\\(m,6)=1}}C_m(h)R_{a_m}.                    \tag{C14}
\]
Since every anchor exceeds \(h\), for \(k\le h\) each \(R_{a_m}(k)=k\), and expansion of \(R_n(k)=k-n\lfloor k/n\rfloor\) yields
\[
F_h(k)=\sum_{n\le k}\mu(n)\lfloor k/n\rfloor=1.                   \tag{C15}
\]
The last equality follows by grouping divisors and using \(\sum_{d\mid j}\mu(d)=\mathbf1_{j=1}\).

Its mean is retained exactly:
\[
m(F_h)=\sum_{n\le h}\frac{\mu(n)}n
\log\frac{a_{\operatorname{core}(n)}}n.                            \tag{C16}
\]
There is no claim that this expression automatically tends to 1 at the rate needed below.

Let \(r\) be the largest power of 2 at most \(h\); then \(r\le h<2r\), and
\[
W_h=R_{3r}-R_{2r}=A_r^{(3)}-A_r^{(2)}\in\mathcal V_h
\]
vanishes through \(h\). Moreover,
\[
m(W_h)=\log(3/2),\qquad
\|W_h\|_{\mathcal H}^2=r\|R_3-R_2\|_{\mathcal H}^2.                                  \tag{C17}
\]
The norm identity uses the exact Pareto dilation law.
The constant in (C17) is explicit:

\[
\boxed{\|R_3-R_2\|^2=\log(3/2)+\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt3}.}
\tag{C17a}
\]

For a proof, \(g=R_3-R_2\) has period-six values \((0,2,-1,1,1,0)\), so \(g^2-g=2\mathbf1_{k\equiv2\text{ or }3\pmod6}\). The mass of these two classes is \([\psi(2/3)-\psi(1/3)]/6=\pi/(6\sqrt3)\), by the digamma reflection identity. Taking means proves (C17a), including the whole infinite tail.

Therefore
\[
\widehat F_h=F_h+
\frac{1-m(F_h)}{\log(3/2)}W_h                                  \tag{C18}
\]
matches the complete head and has mean exactly 1. Its cost is the complete identity
\[
\|1-\widehat F_h\|^2
=\|1-F_h\|^2-2t_h\langle1-F_h,W_h\rangle+t_h^2\|W_h\|^2,
\quad t_h=\frac{1-m(F_h)}{\log(3/2)}.                            \tag{C19}
\]
No covariance or anchor term has been omitted.

### 7.5 A mandatory mean-accuracy condition

For any \(F\in \mathcal H\) satisfying \(F(k)=1\) for \(k\le h\), put \(\delta=1-m(F)\). The tail mass is exactly \(1/(h+1)\). Completion of the square gives
\[
\boxed{
\|1-F\|^2=(h+1)\delta^2+
\sum_{k>h}w_k\bigl(1-F(k)-(h+1)\delta\bigr)^2.}                  \tag{C20}
\]
Consequently bounded errors require \(\delta=O(h^{-1/2})\), and errors tending to zero require \(\delta=o(h^{-1/2})\). Exact head repair by zero-mean corrections, when started from a fixed base of mean different from 1, forces at least linear growth of the squared error. This supplies a direct audit of any proposed infinite head-repair scheme. Equation (C18) repairs the mean exactly, at the full cost (C19).

### 7.6 The exact constrained Gram formula

The graph characterization converts the finite research problem into a completely specified quadratic minimization, retaining the full infinite Pareto tail.

Let \(I=\{2,\ldots,h\}\), \(v_d=-\mu(d)/d\) for \(d\in I\), and use the free set \(B\) from (C11). Define the complete residue Gram matrix and mean vector
\[
G_{de}=\sum_{k\ge1}w_kR_d(k)R_e(k),\qquad \ell_d=\log d.
\]
These are finite matrices of convergent full-tail sums. \(G_{BB}\) is positive definite by residue independence. Let \(A\) have one row per core \(m\le M\), with entries
\(A_{m,d}=\mathbf1_{\operatorname{core}(d)=m}\), \(d\in B\). Let \(c=(C_m(h))_m\). Each component has at least one free vertex, so \(A\) has full row rank.

For simultaneous exact head and exact mean, set
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}A\\\ell_B^T\end{pmatrix},\qquad
y=\begin{pmatrix}c\\1-\ell_I^Tv\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q=G_{BB},\quad q=\ell_B-G_{BI}v,
\]
and
\[
C=1-2\ell_I^Tv+v^TG_{II}v.
\]
The extra mean row is independent of the core rows. Indeed, if \(r\) is the largest power of 2 at most \(M\), both \(2r,3r\) are free vertices of the same core, and their logarithms differ by \(\log(3/2)\). Consequently \(KQ^{-1}K^T\) is positive definite.

For the free residue coefficient vector \(z\), the exact objective and constraints are
\[
\|1-F\|^2=C-2q^Tz+z^TQz,\qquad Kz=y.                          \tag{C21}
\]
Write \(z_0=Q^{-1}q\). Completion of the square under the linear constraints yields
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
d_{h,M}^2={}&C-q^TQ^{-1}q\\
&+(y-Kz_0)^T(KQ^{-1}K^T)^{-1}(y-Kz_0),\\
z_*={}&z_0+Q^{-1}K^T(KQ^{-1}K^T)^{-1}(y-Kz_0).
\end{aligned}}                                                   \tag{C22}
\]
This is the actual minimum over \(F\in\mathcal V_M\) with the entire head through \(h\) correct and mean 1. It is attained uniquely as a function; edge representations can differ by the gauge identities of Section 7.2. To omit the mean condition, use \(K=A,y=c\) instead. Empty fixed sets when \(h=1\) are interpreted by the usual zero-dimensional matrix conventions.

**Proof of (C22).** The unconstrained objective equals \(C-q^TQ^{-1}q+(z-z_0)^TQ(z-z_0)\). The displayed \(z_*\) is feasible, and for any feasible \(z=z_*+u\), \(Ku=0\), so the cross term \(u^TQ(z_*-z_0)\) vanishes. The remaining nonnegative term is \(u^TQu\). This proves both formulas and uniqueness without dropping any signed entries of the original Gram matrix.

The component constraints show precisely what the two seeds accomplish arithmetically. The remaining question is a weighted covariance problem for explicitly prescribed Möbius boundary masses, rather than an unspecified request to repair composites.

### 7.7 The two-parameter exhaustion is the justified limiting formulation

The spaces \(\mathcal V_M\) are nested. Hence \(d_{h,M}^2\) is nonincreasing in \(M\) for each fixed \(h\). No corresponding convergence is claimed for the much more restrictive diagonal sequence \(d_{h,h}^2\).

The map
\[
L_h:\mathcal V_h\to\mathbb R^{h+1},\qquad
F\mapsto(F(1),\ldots,F(h),m(F))
\]
is onto. For arbitrary head data, replace the fixed coefficients in (C11) by \(-\mathcal L_d(f)/d\), including the virtual coefficient \(-f(1)\), and fill each component using one free anchor. This realizes the head. Then add a multiple of \(W_h\) to prescribe the mean independently. Thus \(L_h\) has a linear right inverse with finite-dimensional range in \(\mathcal V_h\).

It follows that
\[
1\in\overline{\bigcup_M\mathcal V_M}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\inf_{M\ge h}d_{h,M}^2=0\quad\text{for every fixed }h\ge1.         \tag{C23}
\]
The forward implication uses a norm-convergent sequence of unrestricted approximants. Their finite evaluation and mean errors tend to zero, and the fixed right inverse corrects those errors by a term whose norm tends to zero. The reverse implication follows already for any one fixed \(h\). All evaluation functionals and the mean are continuous on \(\mathcal H\).

Invoking the **previously established** two-seed closure criterion from the project's *RH Two-Seed Threshold*, version 2, identifies the left side of (C23) with RH. Thus (C22)-(C23) provide an exact, fully specified finite-matrix research target equivalent to that known criterion. This is a reformulation with explicit arithmetic constraints, not a proof of the remaining convergence. Under that criterion, one may choose \(h_j\to\infty\) and an adaptive budget \(M_j\ge h_j\) with error tending to zero; no bound on the necessary budget is supplied.

### 7.8 Mixed-packet legality at composite indices

Here \(J_q=\sum_{d\mid q}\mu(q/d)R_d\), so \(R_n=\sum_{q\mid n}J_q\) and \(m(J_q)=\Lambda(q)\). A mixed packet has a conductor with at least two distinct prime factors and therefore mean zero. There is a useful separate finite construction which must not be confused with the full two-seed closure. Let \(b\) be prime and write \(n=b^vm\), \((b,m)=1\). Möbius inversion between residues and packets gives
\[
A_n^{(b)}=R_{bn}-R_n=\sum_{d\mid m}J_{b^{v+1}d}.                 \tag{C24}
\]
Its only pure-prime-power packet is \(J_{b^{v+1}}\). Therefore if
\(v_b(E)=v_b(n)\), the difference \(A_E^{(b)}-A_n^{(b)}\) is a combination solely of mixed packets. It has mean zero. This criterion remains valid for composite \(n\), including prime powers.

For example,
\[
U(n,3n)(k)=\frac{A_{3n}^{(2)}(k)-A_n^{(2)}(k)}n
=J_6(\lfloor k/n\rfloor).                                    \tag{C25}
\]
This direction is zero before \(n\), equals \(-1\) at \(n\), and has mean zero. Thus the first \(h\) dilates in (C25) form a triangular system that can prescribe any finite head while preserving the starting mean. This also corrects every finite composite defect.

However, the family in (C25) lies in the binary-seed dilation space and inherits its artificial transform zeros. Its finite triangular solvability is not a successful infinite construction. Formula (C24) is a legality tool; the graph completion and the full two-seed matrix (C22) are the broader family that avoids retaining the old binary-only invariant by construction.

### 9.1 The precise remaining problem

With the matrices and vectors in Section 7.6, define

\[
\mathcal E_{h,M}
=C-q^TQ^{-1}q
+(y-KQ^{-1}q)^T(KQ^{-1}K^T)^{-1}(y-KQ^{-1}q).
\]

This is exactly \(d_{h,M}^2\). Every term comes from the complete Pareto Gram matrix; the arithmetic input is the explicitly prescribed Möbius mass at each \(6\)-free core. The remaining approximation target is

\[
\boxed{\text{For each fixed }h,\qquad
\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathcal E_{h,M}=0.}
\tag{R1}
\]

The equivalence between (R1) and membership of \(1\) in the joint two-seed closure was proved in Section 7.7. Identifying that membership with RH invokes the earlier *RH Two-Seed Threshold*, version 2. Accordingly, (R1) is an explicitly parameterized form of the unresolved target, not an additional theorem established by writing down the inverse matrices.

One can equivalently exhibit feasible boundary coefficients \(z_{h,M}\) whose full objective in (C21) tends to zero. That would give a constructive upper bound without explicitly inverting the matrices. In either formulation the two seeds must be optimized jointly: (D13) describes a compulsory cancellation, not a dispensable nuisance term.

### 9.2 What a subsequent estimate must and need not accomplish

The head length and dilation budget must remain independent until a growth estimate has been proved. Fixed-head approximation allows a bounded finite-dimensional correction of head and mean errors. Letting the head grow at the same time changes the norm of that correction; no assertion about \(M=h\), \(M=Ch\), or another predetermined schedule follows from density alone.

The adjacent-repair estimates provide a rigorous model of how summation by parts can control a collective coefficient direction. For the actual target, however, the required coefficients are signed Möbius boundary flows. Their monotonicity or membership in the sufficient class of Section 6 has not been proved. The useful next calculation is a bound for their **combined** signed Gram form, or an exact elimination of one channel followed by a bound for the resulting constrained quadratic form.

The original binary prime-repair span remains useful for studying reverse projections and the arithmetic excess in (T4.11), but its positive floor (F5) forbids completing the RH target inside that span. The all-index two-seed family solves the finite arithmetic problem while exposing the remaining analytic cost. Adding a third independent seed is another research question; nothing here shows that it removes the global difficulty.

## Appendix A. Reproduce the constrained Gram table

This complete Python script uses NumPy and SciPy. Save it as `finite_gram_numeric.py` and run it with Python; it writes the diagnostics beside the script. The two solution methods are checked against each other.

```python
"""Illustrative full-Pareto-tail constrained Gram optimizations; no Lean.

Run with the Codex primary runtime Python (NumPy and SciPy).
Every pair of residue moments uses the exact digamma formula for its own
least-common-multiple period. Floating point is not interval certification.
"""
import json
import math
from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
from scipy import linalg, special


def mobius(n):
    ans, p = 1, 2
    while p * p <= n:
        if n % p == 0:
            n //= p
            ans = -ans
            if n % p == 0:
                return 0
        p += 1
    return -ans if n > 1 else ans


def core(n):
    for p in (2, 3):
        while n % p == 0:
            n //= p
    return n


def vertices(budget):
    return sorted(set(range(1, budget + 1)) |
                  {2 * n for n in range(1, budget + 1)} |
                  {3 * n for n in range(1, budget + 1)})


moment_cache = {}


def gram_entry(a, b):
    a, b = min(a, b), max(a, b)
    if (a, b) not in moment_cache:
        period = math.lcm(a, b)
        r = np.arange(1, period + 1, dtype=np.int64)
        x = r.astype(float) / period
        weights = (special.digamma(x + 1.0 / period) -
                   special.digamma(x)) / period
        moment_cache[a, b] = float(weights @ ((r % a) * (r % b)))
    return moment_cache[a, b]


def gram(indices):
    return np.array([[gram_entry(a, b) for b in indices] for a in indices])


def run(head, budget):
    all_vertices = vertices(budget)
    indices = [n for n in all_vertices if n > 1]
    fixed = [n for n in indices if n <= head]
    free = [n for n in indices if n > head]
    cores = sorted({core(n) for n in all_vertices})
    G = gram(indices)
    positions = {n: i for i, n in enumerate(indices)}
    ii = [positions[n] for n in fixed]
    ib = [positions[n] for n in free]
    v = np.array([-mobius(n) / n for n in fixed])
    logs_fixed = np.log(fixed)
    logs_free = np.log(free)
    Q = G[np.ix_(ib, ib)]
    q = logs_free - G[np.ix_(ib, ii)] @ v
    C = 1 - 2 * logs_fixed @ v + v @ G[np.ix_(ii, ii)] @ v
    A = np.array([[float(core(n) == m) for n in free] for m in cores])
    c = np.array([sum(mobius(n) / n for n in range(1, head + 1)
                      if core(n) == m) for m in cores])
    K = np.vstack((A, logs_free))
    y = np.concatenate((c, [1 - logs_fixed @ v]))

    # Diagonal normalization of the complete Gram quadratic.
    scales = np.sqrt(np.diag(Q))
    Qn = Q / np.outer(scales, scales)
    qn = q / scales
    Kn = K / scales
    row_scales = np.linalg.norm(Kn, axis=1)
    Kn /= row_scales[:, None]
    yn = y / row_scales
    chol = linalg.cho_factor(Qn, lower=True)
    u0 = linalg.cho_solve(chol, qn)
    inverse_K = linalg.cho_solve(chol, Kn.T)
    Schur = Kn @ inverse_K
    correction = linalg.solve(Schur, yn - Kn @ u0, assume_a='pos')
    u = u0 + inverse_K @ correction
    z = u / scales
    value_schur = (C - qn @ u0 +
                   (yn - Kn @ u0) @ correction)

    # Independent null-space route to the same constrained optimum.
    Z = linalg.null_space(Kn)
    up = Kn.T @ linalg.solve(Kn @ Kn.T, yn, assume_a='pos')
    reduced = Z.T @ Qn @ Z
    if Z.shape[1]:
        t = linalg.solve(reduced, Z.T @ (qn - Qn @ up), assume_a='pos')
        un = up + Z @ t
    else:
        un = up
    zn = un / scales

    beta = np.zeros(len(indices))
    beta[ii] = v
    beta[ib] = z
    beta2 = beta.copy()
    beta2[ib] = zn
    mean = np.log(indices) @ beta
    value_gram = float(1 - 2 * mean + beta @ G @ beta)
    value_null = float(1 - 2 * np.log(indices) @ beta2 + beta2 @ G @ beta2)
    head_values = np.array([[k % d for d in indices]
                           for k in range(1, head + 1)]) @ beta
    gradient = Qn @ u - qn
    result = dict(
        head=head, budget=budget, maximum_conductor=max(indices),
        residue_dimension=len(indices), free_residues=len(free),
        linear_constraints=len(y), feasible_dimension=int(Z.shape[1]),
        squared_error=value_gram, schur_value=float(value_schur),
        nullspace_value=value_null,
        schur_gram_difference=float(abs(value_schur-value_gram)),
        nullspace_gram_difference=float(abs(value_null-value_gram)),
        coefficient_method_difference=float(np.max(np.abs(z-zn))),
        raw_gram_condition=float(np.linalg.cond(Q)),
        normalized_gram_condition=float(np.linalg.cond(Qn)),
        normalized_schur_condition=float(np.linalg.cond(Schur)),
        reduced_gram_condition=(float(np.linalg.cond(reduced))
                                if Z.shape[1] else 1.0),
        head_residual=float(np.max(np.abs(head_values-1))),
        mean_residual=float(abs(mean-1)),
        constraint_residual=float(np.max(np.abs(K @ z-y))),
        projected_gradient_residual=float(np.linalg.norm(Z.T @ gradient)),
        smallest_normalized_gram_eigenvalue=float(np.linalg.eigvalsh(Qn)[0]),
    )
    return result


results = [run(head, budget) for head in (1, 4, 8) for budget in (8, 16, 32)]
g_constant = gram_entry(3, 3) + gram_entry(2, 2) - 2 * gram_entry(2, 3)
exact_constant = math.log(1.5) + math.pi / (3 * math.sqrt(3))
payload = dict(
    method='Full infinite Pareto tail via pairwise digamma periodic weights; ordinary float64, not interval certified.',
    constant_numeric=g_constant, constant_exact_formula=exact_constant,
    constant_difference=abs(g_constant-exact_constant),
    pairwise_moments=len(moment_cache), results=results,
)
out = Path(__file__).with_name('finite_gram_numeric.json')
out.write_text(json.dumps(payload, indent=2) + '\n')
print(json.dumps(payload, indent=2))
```

## Appendix B. Reproduce the dual-vector checks

This script uses NumPy. Its explicit tail bounds are mathematical bounds for truncation; it does not certify floating-point roundoff.

```python
"""Illustrative floating-point checks with explicit analytic tail bounds.

This is not interval arithmetic. The reported tail enclosures concern the
truncated formulas; floating-point rounding has not been certified.
"""
from pathlib import Path
import json
import math
import numpy as np


def check(t: float, multiplier: int = 1000):
    cutoff = math.ceil(multiplier * t)
    d_head = 1.0
    derivative_head = 0.0
    for start in range(1, cutoff + 1, 250_000):
        k = np.arange(start, min(start + 250_000, cutoff + 1), dtype=float)
        z = np.log1p(1 / k)
        d_head += float(4 * np.sum(np.sin(t * z / 2) ** 2))

        # h_t=(k+1)k^(it) g/(1-it), g=1-exp((-1+it)z).
        # Phase-centering removes i log(t) h_t from its derivative.
        exponent = (-1 + 1j * t) * z
        exponential = np.exp(exponent)
        g = -np.expm1(exponent)
        bracket = (np.log(k / t) + 1 / (1 - 1j * t)) * g - z * exponential
        derivative_head += float(np.sum(
            (k + 1) / (k * (1 + t * t)) * np.abs(bracket) ** 2
        ))

    # D tail: 2(1-cos y)<=y^2; log(1+1/k)<=1/k;
    # sum_{k>K} k^-2<=1/K.
    d_tail_bound = t * t / cutoff
    # q'_t tail: conditional-expectation contraction on each cell.
    lower_x = cutoff + 1
    log_ratio = math.log(lower_x / t)
    derivative_tail_bound = (log_ratio**2 + 2 * log_ratio + 2) / lower_x
    return {
        "t": t,
        "cutoff": cutoff,
        "D_over_t_lower": d_head / t,
        "D_over_t_upper": (d_head + d_tail_bound) / t,
        "t_phase_derivative_norm2_lower": t * derivative_head,
        "t_phase_derivative_norm2_upper": t * (derivative_head + derivative_tail_bound),
        "D_tail_bound": d_tail_bound,
        "phase_derivative_tail_bound": derivative_tail_bound,
    }


if __name__ == "__main__":
    rows = [check(float(t)) for t in (10, 100, 1000, 10000)]
    out = Path(__file__).with_name("dual_numerics.json")
    out.write_text(json.dumps(rows, indent=2) + "\n")
    for row in rows:
        print(json.dumps(row))
```
