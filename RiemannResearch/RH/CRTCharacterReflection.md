# What the CRT character reflection computes in the Pareto space

Write $P(n)=1/[n(n+1)]$ for $n\ge1$. Let $H=L^2(P)$, $V$ be the closed span of the residue packets, and $M$ the closed span of packets with at least two distinct prime factors. At a positive site, $Rf(n)=f(-n)$ uses the periodic extension of a finite packet sum. Let $S$ be the anchored backward shift, with $Sf(1)=0$ and $Sf(n)=f(n-1)$ for $n\ge2$.

## Reflection on a fixed prime support

For every $q>1$, the identity $[j]_d+[d-1-j]_d=d-1$ on each residue class and Möbius inversion give

$$ R J_q=\varphi(q)\mathbf1-SJ_q . $$

This holds at every positive site, including site 1. Let $p\mid q$ be prime, $m=p^k$, and $f_k=m^{-1}J_{mq}$. The repeated-prime recurrence in the character note gives

$$ f_k(n)=J_q(\lfloor n/m\rfloor),\qquad R f_k=\varphi(q)\mathbf1-S f_k. $$

Summing $P$ over each floor block telescopes exactly. Consequently,

$$
 \|f_k\|_P^2=p^{-k}\|J_q\|_P^2,\qquad
 \|Rf_k-\varphi(q)\mathbf1\|_P^2+
 \sum_{n\ge1}(P(n)-P(n+1)) f_k(n)^2
 =p^{-k}\|J_q\|_P^2.
$$

For $q=6,p=2$, the right side is $2^{-k}\pi/\sqrt3$ and the reflected limit is $2\mathbf1$. The conductors $6\cdot2^k$ keep exactly the primes 2 and 3.

## Why the odd semiprimes alone cannot saturate the mixed projection

At site 2, every packet $J_{2m}$ with odd $m>1$ vanishes. Indeed $J_m(2)=2J_m(1)$, since every divisor of $m$ other than 1 exceeds 2. The prime recurrence gives $J_{2m}(2)=2J_m(1)-J_m(2)=0$. The target $J_2/\log 2-1$ equals $-1$ there. It follows that every trial supported on these conductors has complete squared error at least $1/6$ under $P$ and at least $1/(4\zeta(2))$ under $Q$. Allowing all odd primes $p$ in $J_{2p}$ therefore leaves the same obstruction. The full mixed dictionary also contains conductors such as 12, and $J_{12}(2)=-2$.

## The graph on mixed packets

Form the closed subspace $G\subset H\times H$ generated by $(J_q,RJ_q)$ over mixed conductors. Its exact description is

$$ (x,y)\in G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in M\quad\text{and}\quad y+Sx\in\mathbb R\mathbf1 . $$

The right side is closed and contains every generator. The conductor-six tower puts $(0,2\mathbf1)$ in $G$. Subtracting a multiple of this vertical vector from each generator gives $(J_q,-SJ_q)$. Continuity of the backward shift gives $(x,-Sx)$ for all $x\in M$. Adding the vertical constants gives the whole right side. Thus the closure of the graph has exactly one free scalar at every $x\in M$.

## Where RH enters

The target is membership of $\mathbf1$ in $V$. If $G\subseteq M\times V$, its vertical fibre gives $\mathbf1\in V$. Conversely, the established RH equivalence gives $V=H$ under RH. Along any one fixed tower as above, cofinal membership $Rf_k\in V$ also suffices, by strong convergence and $\varphi(q)>0$.

Deleting all pure powers of a specified prime from the original packet dictionary leaves its closure equal to $V$. More generally, for any selection containing every mixed conductor, its closure is $M$ if it retains no prime power, and $V$ if it retains at least one. This follows from the mean $\langle J_q,\mathbf1\rangle_P=\Lambda(q)$ and the equality $M=V\cap\mathbf1^\perp$.

With $e_q$ the conductor-ordered orthonormal mixed packet basis and $u=(I-P_M)J_2/\log 2$, the established character-Gram balance reads

$$
 \sum_{q\ \mathrm{mixed}} |\langle J_2,e_q\rangle_P|^2
 + (\log 2)^2\|u\|_P^2=\log 2.
$$

The remaining claim is that the sum equals $\log 2-(\log 2)^2$, equivalently $\|u\|_P^2=1$. The reflected limit belongs to the ambient second coordinate of $G$. Proving that this coordinate lies in $V$ would settle the claim.
