## 1. Setting and indexing

Use the real Hilbert space with physical weights
\[
Q(n)=\frac1{\zeta(2)n^2},\qquad n\ge1,
\]
and squarefree legal atoms \(g_d(n)=(n\bmod d)/d\), \(d\ge2\).
The actual masked stage is generated by
\[
a_i=\mathbf1_{\mathrm{Squarefree}(i+2)}g_{i+2},\qquad i<N.
\]
Thus its largest possible conductor is \(N+1\), and inactive coordinates are zero. The masked Gram matrix may be singular.

Write
\[
P_N=\Pi_N,\quad F_N=P_N1,\quad r_N=1-F_N,
\quad \varepsilon_N=\|r_N\|_Q^2,\quad
Bf(n)=\frac{f(n)}{n+1},\quad b_N=P_NBr_N.
\]
The residuals converge in the complete Hilbert norm to \(r=1-P_V1\). In particular, they are uniformly tight for complete physical tails:
\[
\forall\delta>0\ \exists H_0\ \forall H\ge H_0\ \forall N,
\qquad \|\operatorname{tail}_H r_N\|_Q<\delta.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{tail}_H\) retains **every** physical site strictly greater than \(H\). This assertion supplies no quantitative rate and no bound after multiplication by \(H\).

The existing RH consumer requires a common constant in
\[
\exists C\ge0\ \forall N,n,\qquad
1\le n\le N\Longrightarrow |b_N(n)|\le C.
\tag{R2}
\]

## 2. Masked first-site decomposition and choice independence

Let \(G_{ij}=\langle a_i,a_j\rangle_Q\), \(Gc=m\) be a normal system for \(F_N\), and
\[
\rho_i=\langle a_i,Br_N\rangle_Q,
\qquad t=1-B1,\qquad A_N=\langle r_N,t\rangle_Q.
\]
Define the masked leading vector and remainder by
\[
u_i=\frac{\mathbf1_{\mathrm{Squarefree}(i+2)}}{i+2},
\qquad \eta_i=\rho_i-A_Nu_i.
\]
The first-site identity is exact:
\[
u_i=a_i(1).
\]
For the physical evaluation vector \(e_n\), characterized by
\(\langle f,e_n\rangle_Q=f(n)\), put \(k_{N,n}=P_Ne_n\).
If \(Gx=(a_i(n))_i\), then the synthesized vector is \(\sum_i x_i a_i=k_{N,n}\). Consequently,
\[
\sum_i x_i u_i=k_{N,n}(1),
\qquad
\boxed{b_N(n)=A_Nk_{N,n}(1)+\sum_i x_i\eta_i.}
\tag{2}
\]

Both separated systems are solvable even for singular masked \(G\): their right-hand sides arise by testing the ambient vectors \(e_1\) and \(Br_N-A_Ne_1\). Inactive coordinates vanish. The remainder vector is independent of the normal coefficients \(c\), and both scalar responses in (2) are independent of the chosen solution \(x\). These statements concern synthesized vectors and scalar responses; they do not assert uniqueness of masked coefficients.

The implementation is in `RH/QSquarefreeForcingDecomposition.lean`, notably `leadingCoordinate_eq_evaluation`, `leading_response`, `signed_decomposition`, `remainder_eq_inner`, `exists_separated_systems`, and the choice-independence declarations.

## 3. A sharper, tail-sensitive forcing estimate

For every positive conductor, including conductors outside a finite stage, define
\[
E_d=dBg_d-t.
\]
The exact physical identity is
\[
E_d(n)=-\frac{d\lfloor n/d\rfloor}{n+1}.
\]
It implies support on \(n\ge d\) and the sharper bound
\[
|E_d(n)|\le\frac n{n+1}.
\]
Thus, on that support,
\[
\zeta(2)Q(n)|E_d(n)|^2
\le\frac1{(n+1)^2}
\le\frac1{n(n+1)}.
\]
Summing the complete telescoping majorant gives
\[
\boxed{\zeta(2)\|E_d\|_Q^2\le\frac1d.}
\tag{3}
\]
This improves the previous constant \(2/d\).

Put \(\rho_{N,d}=\langle r_N,Bg_d\rangle_Q\). Because \(E_d\) is supported on \(n\ge d\),
\[
d\rho_{N,d}-A_N
=\langle\operatorname{tail}_{d-1}r_N,E_d\rangle_Q.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz and (3) therefore give
\[
\boxed{\zeta(2)d\,(d\rho_{N,d}-A_N)^2
\le\|\operatorname{tail}_{d-1}r_N\|_Q^2.}
\tag{4}
\]
For an active coordinate, \(d\rho_{N,d}-A_N=d\eta_{N,d}\).
Uniform tightness (1) upgrades (4) to the precise rate-free assertion
\[
\forall\epsilon>0\ \exists D\ \forall d\ge D\ \forall N,
\qquad \zeta(2)d\,(d\rho_{N,d}-A_N)^2\le\epsilon.
\tag{5}
\]
Equivalently, the remainder is \(o(d^{-3/2})\), uniformly in the moving stage. This controls the forcing before the Gram solve; it is not an estimate for \(b_N(n)\).

The relevant sources are `RH/QSmoothedResidueAsymptotic.lean` and `RH/QUniformTail.lean`; the principal statements are `atomError_energy_le`, `signed_forcing_tail_sq_bound`, and `uniform_tail_tightness_of_tendsto`. The finite-stage theorem `residual_forcing_uniform_little_o` is in `RH/QSquarefreeForcingDecomposition.lean`.

## 4. Joint forcing control through discrete Hardy

The Lean-verified complete-space estimate, uniformly for every finite conductor cutoff \(D\), is
\[
\boxed{\zeta(2)\sum_{d=1}^{D}
\left|d\langle r,Bg_d\rangle_Q-\langle r,t\rangle_Q\right|^2
\le4\|r\|_Q^2,\qquad r\in H_Q.}
\tag{6}
\]
For \(r=r_N\), restricting to active squarefree coordinates gives
\(\zeta(2)\sum d^2|\eta_{N,d}|^2\le4\varepsilon_N\).
This joint bound is stronger than summing the individual estimates, whose corresponding upper bounds have a harmonic divergence.

Here is the mathematical proof, including the endpoint. In unweighted Hilbert coordinates, let \(a_n=|r_{n-1}|\) for \(n\ge1\). Formula (3)'s pointwise argument gives
\[
\sqrt{\zeta(2)}\,|\langle r,E_d\rangle_Q|
\le\sum_{n\ge d}\frac{a_n}{n+1}.
\]
For a finite input supported on \(1,\ldots,M\), write
\(s_d=\sum_{n=d}^M a_n/(n+1)\), with \(s_{M+1}=0\).
Weighted telescoping yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{d=1}^M s_d^2
&=\sum_{d=1}^M d(s_d^2-s_{d+1}^2)\\
&=\sum_{d=1}^M\frac{d a_d}{d+1}(s_d+s_{d+1})
\le2\sum_{d=1}^M a_d s_d.
\end{aligned}
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\sum s_d^2\le4\sum a_d^2\).
Apply this to finite coordinate heads of \(r\), and pass to the limit for each fixed finite output cutoff \(D\). The finite-head approximations converge in the complete Hilbert norm, so every inner product on the left converges. This proves (6) without truncating its Hilbert-space inner products in the final statement.

The modules are `Analysis/DiscreteTailHardy.lean` and `RH/QSmoothedResidueBessel.lean`, with the latter retaining namespace `RH.QSmoothedResidueAsymptotic`. The application declarations are `atomError_finite_bessel` and `signed_forcing_finite_bessel`. The complete conductor sum is also checked: `atomError_pairing_square_summable` and `atomError_bessel` prove summability and
\[
\zeta(2)\sum_{d\ge1}|\langle r,E_d\rangle_Q|^2\le4\|r\|_Q^2.
\]

The consequence
\[
\left|\sum_i x_i\eta_i\right|
\le 2\sqrt{\varepsilon_N/\zeta(2)}
\left(\sum_{i\text{ active}}\frac{x_i^2}{d_i^2}\right)^{1/2}
\]
does not settle (R2): no uniform estimate for the last inverse-response norm has been proved. It also discards the signed relation to the first-site response in (2).

## 5. Localization leaves a finite lower-site response

For a positive physical evaluation site \(n\), set
\[
\beta_n=\frac1{n+1},\qquad
u_{N,n}=(B-\beta_n I)k_{N,n}.
\]
Orthogonality gives \(b_N(n)=\langle r_N,u_{N,n}\rangle_Q\).
The physical diagonal of \(u_{N,n}\) vanishes. Split the exact response as
\[
b_N(n)=L_N(n)+U_N(n),
\]
where the finite signed lower-site sum is
\[
\boxed{L_N(n)=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}Q(m)r_N(m)
\left(\frac1{m+1}-\frac1{n+1}\right)k_{N,n}(m),}
\tag{7}
\]
and \(U_N(n)\) is the complete contribution from \(m\ge n\).

The evaluation bound \(\|k_{N,n}\|_Q^2\le\zeta(2)n^2\), together with
\(|\beta_m-\beta_n|\le\beta_n\) for \(m\ge n\), proves
\[
\|\operatorname{tail}_{n-1}u_{N,n}\|_Q^2
\le\zeta(2)\frac{n^2}{(n+1)^2}\le\zeta(2),
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{|U_N(n)|^2\le\zeta(2)
\|\operatorname{tail}_{n-1}r_N\|_Q^2.}
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, \(U_N(n)\to0\) as \(n\to\infty\), **uniformly in \(N\)**. The unproved physical regularity is confined to (7), rather than to the complete upper tail.

The residualized kernel energy retains its full Schur subtraction:
\[
\boxed{\Lambda_{N,n}
=\|\operatorname{head}_{n-1}u_{N,n}\|_Q^2
+\|\operatorname{tail}_{n-1}u_{N,n}\|_Q^2
-\|P_Nu_{N,n}\|_Q^2.}
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(\Lambda_{N,n}\le\|\operatorname{head}_{n-1}u_{N,n}\|_Q^2+\zeta(2)\), but this weaker positive bound may lose essential cancellation.

These declarations are in `RH/QShiftedReciprocalTail.lean` and `RH/QSquarefreeKernelLocalization.lean`: `centeredKernel_upperTail_energy_le`, `movingCorrection_lowerHead_upperTail`, `movingCorrection_lowerHead_remainder_sq_le`, `lowerHead_response_uniform_error`, and `kernelEnergy_lowerHead_upperTail_exact`.
The Lean localization parameter is zero-based: its physical evaluation site is `n + 1`, and `tail n` retains physical sites at least `n + 1`. Equations (7)–(9) use positive physical indexing instead.

## 7. Remaining research agenda

1. **Primary signed lower-site estimate.** Prove a common \(C\) for \(|L_N(n)|\) when \(1\le n\le N\). Equation (8) then controls the entire omitted contribution, giving (R2). Retain the signs of \(r_N(m)k_{N,n}(m)\); the multiplier in (7) is positive for \(m<n\), but the remaining factors have no established common sign. Combine normal-system identities with the exact floor-function forcing, rather than treating the Bessel remainder as arbitrary data.

2. **Kernel alternative.** Prove a uniform bound for \(\varepsilon_N\Lambda_{N,n}\) over the same heads. Equation (9) already controls the upper term; the open work is the lower-site energy together with the projected subtraction. Bounding the lower positive term alone is a stronger sufficient approach, not a known necessary condition.

3. **Use joint forcing without losing its correlation.** The endpoint estimate (6) is unconditional, but weighted Cauchy–Schwarz still introduces an uncontrolled inverse-response norm. A useful next estimate must use the actual normal forcing and evaluation response together, or exhibit a cancellation mechanism valid even at sites where the two terms in (2) reinforce.

4. **Matched finite displacement (R4) remains open.** The established equation \((I-P_NB)d_N=b_N\) has a controlled Hilbert inverse, but no physical supremum estimate follows from that fact. The existing \(\|d_N\|_Q^2\le\varepsilon_N/8\) controls Hilbert energy; it does not supply physical arithmetic regularity. No common displacement head bound was proved here.

5. **Cofinal complete tails (R5) remain open.** Strong convergence lets one reduce an approximation error after choosing a cutoff; it does not simultaneously bound the cutoff-scaled finite approximant tail. Each finite correction is periodic, so its scaled tail has a periodic mean-square limit at large cutoffs. Even a uniform bound on those eventual limits would require additional control of the onset and approximation rate to produce a valid diagonal sequence. Neither that compatibility nor the required common scaled-tail bound was established.

The acceptance criterion remains an unconditional arithmetic provider with the stated quantifiers, followed by the existing Lean implication to Mathlib's exact `RiemannHypothesis`. The forcing improvements and the localization of the remaining response are partial progress, not completion of that criterion.
