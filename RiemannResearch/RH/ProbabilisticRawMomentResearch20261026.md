## 1. Objects and the existing residual criterion

Let \(X\) have the fixed physical law
\[
Q(n)=\frac1{\zeta(2)n^2},\qquad n\ge1.
\]
For the actual squarefree stages and Q-orthogonal projections, write
\[
r_N=(I-P_N)1,\quad s_N=Br_N,\quad b_N=P_Ns_N,
\quad Bf(n)=\frac{f(n)}{n+1},\quad e_N=\|r_N\|_Q^2\le1.
\]
The existing theory gives \(r_N\to r\), \(b_N\to b=P_VBr\) in Q,
\(r=0\Rightarrow\mathrm{RH}\), and \(\|b_N\|_Q\le\|r_N\|_Q/4\).
Set \(a(0)=0\), \(a(n)=n/(8+\log n)^2\) for \(n\ge1\), and define
\[
Z_N=b_N(X)(s_N(X)-b_N(X)),\qquad
M_N(t)=\mathbb E_Q[Z_N\mathbf1_{\{X\le t\}}],
\]
\[
\Phi_N^\pm(H)=\sum_{t<H}\Delta a(t)[\pm M_N(t)]_+,
\qquad c_H(n)=a(\min(n,H)).
\]
Projection orthogonality gives \(\mathbb E_Q Z_N=0\). The capped layer energy is
\[
X_N(H)=\mathbb E_Q[c_H(X)b_N(X)^2]
=\sum_{n\le H}a(n)Q(n)b_N(n)^2+a(H)\|T_Hb_N\|_Q^2.
\]
The established exact criterion is
\[
r=0\iff\exists C\ \forall H\ \forall N_0\ \exists N\ge N_0:
\Phi_N^+(H)\le C.
\tag{1}
\]
The same constant serves every head; late stages may depend on the head.

## 2. A genuine martingale and a fixed-Q expectation

Let \(\mathcal F_t\) be generated by the singleton events \(X=1,\ldots,X=t\)
and the remaining tail \(X>t\). For each stage,
\[
L_{N,t}=\mathbb E_Q[Z_N\mid\mathcal F_t]
\]
is a closed integrable martingale. If \(q_t=Q(X>t)>0\), its value on the
tail event is \(-M_N(t)/q_t\). Thus exactly
\[
\Phi_N^+(H)=\sum_{t<H}\Delta a(t)
\mathbb E_Q[(-L_{N,t})_+\mathbf1_{\{X>t\}}].
\]
The legal projection \(P_N\) itself is not asserted to be a conditional
expectation: its range lacks the constant target and need not be an algebra.

Define the finite nonnegative expectations
\[
A_{N,H}^\pm=\mathbb E_Q[c_H(X)(\pm Z_N)_+].
\]
Conditional Jensen gives \(\Phi_N^+\le A_{N,H}^-\) and
\(\Phi_N^-\le A_{N,H}^+\). Finite telescoping and \(\mathbb E Z_N=0\) give
the complete signed identity
\[
\boxed{\Phi_N^+(H)-\Phi_N^-(H)=A_{N,H}^--A_{N,H}^+.}
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(Z_N=s_N^2/4-(b_N-s_N/2)^2\) and
\(\mathbb E[a(X)s_N(X)^2]\le e_N/256\),
\[
\boxed{0\le A_{N,H}^--\Phi_N^+(H)
=A_{N,H}^+-\Phi_N^-(H)\le e_N/1024.}
\tag{3}
\]
Hence the positive-moment budget is within a small uniform additive error
of a natural nonnegative expectation under the original Q law.

## 3. Stronger additive control from source smoothing

For \(\beta\ge0\), \(|[\beta-\gamma]_+-\beta|\le|\gamma|\). Apply this
to the exact tail identity
\(M_N(t)=\|T_tb_N\|_Q^2-\langle T_tb_N,T_ts_N\rangle_Q\), then sum:
\[
|\Phi_N^+(H)-X_N(H)|
\le\mathbb E_Q[c_H(X)|b_N(X)s_N(X)|].
\]
The crucial extra fact is that \(aB\) is a bounded multiplier:
\[
0\le\frac{c_H(n)}{n+1}\le\frac{a(n)}{n+1}\le\frac1{64}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and the quarter contraction therefore prove
\[
\boxed{|\Phi_N^+(H)-X_N(H)|\le\frac{e_N}{256}\le\frac1{256}.}
\tag{4}
\]
Alternatively use \(|br|\le2b^2+r^2/8\) to avoid square roots. This improves
the previous \(\sqrt{e_NX_N(H)}/16\) estimate when the energy is large.
The full signed decomposition remains
\[
\Phi_N^+(H)-X_N(H)=\Phi_N^-(H)
-\mathbb E_Q[c_H(X)b_N(X)s_N(X)].
\tag{5}
\]
If a fixed \(\theta<1\) and \(C\) satisfy
\(\Phi_N^+(H)\le\theta X_N(H)+C\), then
\[
X_N(H)\le\frac{C+e_N/256}{1-\theta}
\le\frac{C+1/256}{1-\theta}.
\tag{6}
\]
A cofinal such estimate on expanding heads forces \(r=0\), hence RH.
The arithmetic strict-gap hypothesis is still unproved.

## 4. Uniform integrability: the controlled sign and the unresolved sign

On the fixed Q space, set
\[
U_N=a(X)(Z_N)_+,\qquad Y_N=a(X)(-Z_N)_+.
\]
Unconditionally,
\[
0\le U_N\le a(X)s_N(X)^2/4\le r_N(X)^2/1024.
\]
Because \(r_N\to r\) in \(L^2(Q)\), the squares converge in \(L^1(Q)\)
and are uniformly integrable. Consequently **\(\{U_N\}\) is uniformly
integrable unconditionally**. The remaining sign is \(Y_N\).

Let \(s=Br\), \(b=P_Vs\), and
\(Y=a(X)[b(X)^2-b(X)s(X)]_+\). Coordinate convergence gives
\(Y_N\to Y\) almost surely. The existing weighted-energy criterion implies
\[
\boxed{r=0\iff\mathbb E_QY<\infty.}
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed \(b^2\le2[b^2-bs]_++s^2\); finite expectation in (7) implies finite
weighted b-energy, forcing \(r=0\). The forward implication gives \(Y=0\).
Thus UI of \(\{Y_N\}\), or bounded expectations along a subsequence,
would close the implication. Neither is established here.

For comparison, \(J_N=a(X)b_N(X)^2\) converges almost surely to the finite
random variable \(J=a(X)b(X)^2\), so \(\{J_N\}\) is already tight.
If \(r\ne0\), however, \(\mathbb E J=\infty\). Tightness and almost-sure
convergence alone therefore cannot provide the missing weighted mean bound.
An exact concentration criterion is
\(r=0\iff b_N(X)\to0\) in probability; its arithmetic proof remains open.

There is an exact slow-head UI criterion:
\[
r=0\iff\exists H_N\to\infty:
\{a(X)b_N(X)^2\mathbf1_{\{X\le H_N\}}\}_N\text{ is UI}.
\tag{8}
\]
For necessity, choose increasing \(N_j\) so that every \(N\ge N_j\) has
head-j energy at most \(1/j\), then set \(H_N=\max\{j:N_j\le N\}\), using zero when the set is empty.
The truncated variables converge to zero in \(L^1\). Conversely Fatou and
UI give finite weighted energy of b. This does not assert UI for every
head schedule or for the full untruncated family.

## 5. An exact use of Kolmogorov's three-series theorem

Set \(d_{N,t}=\Delta a(t)[M_N(t)]_+\), and let
\(d_t=\lim_Nd_{N,t}\), which exists for every fixed t. The limiting
coefficients are deterministic; no diagonal stage choice is made.
On an auxiliary probability space let \(\varepsilon_t\) be independent
symmetric signs. Three-series gives
\[
\sum_t\sqrt{d_t}\varepsilon_t\text{ converges almost surely}
\iff\sum_td_t<\infty\iff r=0.
\tag{9}
\]
For the first equivalence, square summability gives convergence of the
centered independent series. Conversely convergence forces \(d_t\to0\),
after which the three-series truncated variance condition is exactly
\(\sum_td_t<\infty\). Positive probability of convergence suffices by
Kolmogorov's zero-one law. The second equivalence follows from (1) and
fixed-head convergence. Each fixed finite stage already has a convergent
randomized series; the unresolved issue is passage across stages.

Thus three-series applies rigorously, but its variance condition is precisely
the missing arithmetic budget. Auxiliary signs do not prove independence
of the original arithmetic data or provide the variance bound.

## 6. Fourth moments give a one-quantile criterion

Define \(R_{N,H}=\sum_{t<H}\sqrt{d_{N,t}}\varepsilon_t\) and
\(v=\Phi_N^+(H)\). Independence gives exactly
\[
\mathbb ER_{N,H}^2=v,\qquad
\mathbb ER_{N,H}^4=3v^2-2\sum_{t<H}d_{N,t}^2\le3v^2.
\]
For \(v>0\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied above the threshold \(v/2\) gives
\(\Pr(R_{N,H}^2>v/2)\ge1/12\). Hence
\[
\Pr(|R_{N,H}|>K)<1/12\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v\le2K^2.
\tag{10}
\]
One common K satisfying (10) on every fixed head at arbitrarily late stages
therefore forces \(r=0\). No separate UI hypothesis is needed, because the
fourth-moment bound supplies quantitative anti-concentration.

## 7. Bounded coefficients, Gaussian escape, and any retained mass

There is an unconditional coefficient bound \(0\le d_{N,t}\le1/512\).
Indeed \(\Delta a(0)=1/64\), and for \(x\ge1\),
\(a'(x)=(6+\log x)/(8+\log x)^3\in[0,1/64]\), so the mean value theorem
gives \(0\le\Delta a(t)\le1/64\). Orthogonality gives
\[
|M_N(t)|\le\|b_N\|\|s_N-b_N\|
\le\|s_N\|^2/2\le e_N/8.
\]
For \(v>0\), the normalized independent summands of \(R_{N,H}/\sqrt v\)
have total variance one and absolute values at most \(1/\sqrt{512v}\).
Thus whenever \(v_j\to\infty\), the Lindeberg condition holds and
\(R_{N_j,H_j}/\sqrt{v_j}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\).
No independence across rows is needed.

The bounded Berry--Esseen estimate cited below gives, for \(K\ge0\),
\[
\Pr(|R_{N,H}|\le K)
\le\frac{\sqrt{2/\pi}\,K+33/(5\sqrt{512})}{\sqrt v}.
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently the following is an exact paper equivalence:
\[
\boxed{r=0\iff\exists K>0,\ p>0\ \forall H,N_0\ \exists N\ge N_0:
\Pr(|R_{N,H}|\le K)\ge p.}
\tag{12}
\]
The right side bounds \(v\) by
\([\{\sqrt{2/\pi}K+33/(5\sqrt{512})\}/p]^2\), including \(v=0\)
trivially. Necessity uses fixed-head \(v\to0\) and Chebyshev.
The retained mass p can be small, but must be independent of H and N.
If \(r\ne0\), the verified expanding-head divergence and (11) instead give
\(\Pr(|R_{N,H(N)}|\le K)\to0\) for every finite K and every \(H(N)\to\infty\).
The bounded-summand hypothesis matters: arbitrary Rademacher sums can have
large variance and retain an atom at zero.

## 8. Warning about the earlier changing-cutoff representation

For a fixed stage, take \(\Pr(T_H=t)=\Delta a(t)/a(H)\) and
\(W_{N,H}=a(H)[M_N(T_H)]_+\). Although \(\mathbb EW_{N,H}=\Phi_N^+(H)\),
one has \(W_{N,H}\to0\) in probability as \(H\to\infty\), while its mean
tends to \(\Phi_N^+(\infty)\). To prove probability convergence, split
at \(T_H<K\): its probability is \(a(K)/a(H)\), and Markov bounds the
remaining part by \(\epsilon^{-1}\sum_{t\ge K}\Delta a(t)[M_N(t)]_+\).
If the limiting mean is positive, this family is **not UI**, even at one
finite stage. This is an artifact of the changing cutoff laws, not evidence
for or against \(r=0\).

## 9. Actual arithmetic independence and an Efron--Stein provider

The Q law has a genuine product structure. For each prime p, let
\(V_p\) be independent with
\[
\Pr(V_p=k)=(1-p^{-2})p^{-2k},\qquad k\ge0.
\]
Since \(\sum_p\Pr(V_p>0)=\sum_pp^{-2}<\infty\), the first
Borel--Cantelli lemma gives finite support almost surely. Therefore
\(X=\prod_pp^{V_p}\) is a finite positive integer almost surely, and the
Euler product gives
\[
\Pr(X=n)=\left(\prod_p(1-p^{-2})\right)n^{-2}
=\frac1{\zeta(2)n^2}.
\]
This is independence of prime valuations; it does not assert independence
of arbitrary residue classes under Q.

Let \(V_p'\) be an independent copy and define the resampled integer
\(X^{(p)}=(X/p^{V_p})p^{V_p'}\). Set
\[
g_{N,H}(n)=\sqrt{c_H(n)}\,b_N(n),\qquad
D_{N,H}=\frac12\sum_p\mathbb E\!\left[
\bigl(g_{N,H}(X)-g_{N,H}(X^{(p)})\bigr)^2\right].
\]
Each \(g_{N,H}\) belongs to \(L^2(Q)\), since the cap \(c_H\) is bounded.
Countable Efron--Stein gives \(\operatorname{Var}(g_{N,H}(X))\le D_{N,H}\),
allowing the right side to be infinite. One proof enumerates the primes,
applies finite Efron--Stein to the conditional expectation on the first m
valuations, bounds its resampling differences by conditional Jensen, and
passes to the \(L^2\) martingale limit.

Put \(A=\mathbb E_Qa(X)<\infty\). Cauchy--Schwarz and quarter contraction
bound the squared mean by
\((\mathbb Eg_{N,H}(X))^2\le A\|b_N\|_Q^2\le Ae_N/16\). Hence
\[
\boxed{X_N(H)\le D_{N,H}+\frac{Ae_N}{16}.}
\tag{13}
\]
In particular, the following common cofinal resampling bound is sufficient:
\[
\exists C\ \forall H,N_0\ \exists N\ge N_0:\quad D_{N,H}\le C.
\tag{14}
\]
It gives one common finite-head energy bound, hence \(r=0\) and RH by the
existing criterion. This supplies a local arithmetic target involving the
response to changing one prime valuation. No bound (14) is proved here,
and no reverse implication from \(r=0\) to (14) is asserted. Independence
permits Efron--Stein; it does not itself control the resampling differences.

## 10. Remaining arithmetic provider and references

Concrete targets from this analysis include the common retained-mass bound
(12), a cofinal version on expanding heads, or the prime-resampling provider
(14). Other routes are a common expectation bound for the weighted negative
local part, or a strict gap in (6). The additive estimate controls the discrepancy;
it does not bound either growing energy by itself. Standard martingale
convergence, three-series, and normal approximation do not supply this provider.
