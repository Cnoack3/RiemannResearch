# Dual Gram screening and complete-error absorption

Date: 9 November 2026 (session continued on 10 November UTC).

The goal is an unconditional bound forcing the complete Pareto projection error to zero. This change proves signed pairing estimates and conditional upper bounds using the existing residue, Ramanujan, zero-coordinate, reciprocal and displacement objects. It does **not** prove the remaining uniform arithmetic estimate, density, or RH.

## 1. Complete spaces and existing identities

For positive integers, put
```math
P(n)=\frac1{n(n+1)},\qquad Q(n)=\frac1{\zeta(2)n^2}.
```
Physical sequences have value zero at the anchor 0. All norms below include the entire infinite support. At a period \(L\), the legal space is generated by
\(g_d(n)=(n\bmod d)/d\) for every nontrivial divisor \(d\mid L\), including mixed conductors and prime powers. Write \(\Pi_L^P,\Pi_L^Q\) for the corresponding projections. In the LCM parametrization,
```math
L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,M),\quad
N=L-1,\quad f_M=\Pi_L^P\mathbf1,\quad
E_M=\|\mathbf1-f_M\|_P^2.
```
The residue masses and Mellin sums, for \(1\le a\le L\), are
```math
p_a=\sum_{j\ge0}P(a+jL),\qquad
m_a(s)=\sum_{j\ge0}\big((a+jL)^{-s}-(a+jL+1)^{-s}\big).
```
The multiples class is \(a=L\); \(\mu_M=p_L\). For the other classes, let
\(e_a=\sqrt{p_a}(1-f_M(a))\).
For a finite family of actual zeros \(z_i\), \(\Re z_i>0\), \(z_i\ne1\), take both real and imaginary rows
```math
v_{i,\mathrm{re}}(a)=\frac{\Re m_a(z_i)}{\sqrt{p_a}},\qquad
v_{i,\mathrm{im}}(a)=\frac{\Im m_a(z_i)}{\sqrt{p_a}}.
```
Let \(Z\) be their Gram matrix and \(b\) the corresponding real and imaginary parts of \(1-m_L(z_i)\). The exact observation is \(\langle v_i,e\rangle=b_i\).
The orthogonal projection \(\Pi_Ze\) is the canonical zero certificate even if the rows are dependent. Define
```math
C_M=\|\Pi_Ze\|^2,\qquad R_M=\|e-\Pi_Ze\|^2.
```
The repository already proved
```math
E_M=\mu_M+C_M+R_M
   =1-\lambda^{\mathsf T}G_L^{-1}\lambda,
\qquad \lambda_q=\Lambda(q).
```
Here \(G_L\) is the complete Pareto Gram of the Ramanujan packets, indexed by all nontrivial divisors of \(L\). It is not the zero Gram \(Z\). When the zero rows are independent, \(C_M=b^{\mathsf T}Z^{-1}b\).

For a fixed finite family of distinct positive critical ordinates, the existing theorems give \(\mu_M\to0\) and \(C_M\to0\), unconditionally. They do not give \(R_M\to0\).
Existing source modules include `ParetoLCMZeroCertificate`, `ParetoLCMRamanujanCertificate`, `ParetoResidueCriticalNormalLimit`, and `ParetoResidueMovingCertificate`.

## 2. New signed Hilbert-space and inverse-Gram estimates

Module: [ProjectionPairingSplit.lean](Analysis/ProjectionPairingSplit.lean).

For any closed subspace with orthogonal projection \(\Pi\),
```math
\langle x,y\rangle
 =\langle\Pi x,\Pi y\rangle+
   \langle(I-\Pi)x,(I-\Pi)y\rangle,
```
and
```math
|\langle x,y\rangle|
\le |\langle\Pi x,\Pi y\rangle|
   +\|(I-\Pi)x\|\,\|(I-\Pi)y\|
\le\|x\|\,\|y\|.
```
Thus the split bound is never larger than the unsplit Cauchy--Schwarz product. This is not a claim of strict improvement, or monotonic improvement as the test space grows.

For an independent finite family \(v\), with Gram \(G\), \(b_i=\langle v_i,x\rangle\), and \(k_i=\langle v_i,y\rangle\), the literal matrix theorem is
```math
(\langle x,y\rangle-b^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}k)^2
\le
(\|x\|^2-b^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}b)
(\|y\|^2-k^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}k).
```
Both projected-energy subtractions and the signed captured pairing are retained. Independence is an explicit premise only for the inverse-matrix form; the projection form covers singular families.

## 3. Actual residue and zero dual Grams

Module: [ParetoResidueZeroScreening.lean](RH/ParetoResidueZeroScreening.lean).

For any test vector \(h\) in the weighted nonzero residue coordinates, let \(k_i=\langle v_i,h\rangle\). The new theorem `lcm_primal_dual_gram_pairing_bound` proves
```math
(\langle e,h\rangle-b^{\mathsf T}Z^{-1}k)^2
\le
\underbrace{\bigl(1-\lambda^{\mathsf T}G_L^{-1}\lambda
                  -\mu_M-b^{\mathsf T}Z^{-1}b\bigr)}_{R_M}
\bigl(\|h\|^2-k^{\mathsf T}Z^{-1}k\bigr).
```
The two inverses refer to the actual Ramanujan and zero Grams. No surrogate random matrix replaces either one. The other new residue theorems identify the canonical certificate with the projection of every legal error, and provide the signed and squared-remainder forms without an independence premise:
- `certificate_eq_error_projection`
- `zero_test_pairing_split`
- `zero_test_pairing_remainder_sq_le`

## 4. An explicit conditional upper bound for the complete error

The new theorem `lcm_error_le_of_remainder_absorption` proves, at every finite stage,
```math
R_M\le\theta E_M+B_M,\quad\theta<1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E_M\le\frac{\mu_M+C_M+B_M}{1-\theta}.
```
No positivity premise on \(\theta\) is needed. In the intended application \(0\le\theta<1\) and \(B_M\ge0\).

For a fixed finite family of distinct positive critical ordinates, `riemannHypothesis_of_remainder_absorption` proves that an eventual version of this inequality, with fixed \(\theta<1\) and any \(B_M\to0\), implies RH. The proof uses the already established fact that \(E_M\) and \(R_M\) have the same nonnegative limit. Passing to the limit gives \(E_\infty\le\theta E_\infty\), hence \(E_\infty=0\).

The additional theorem `lcm_error_le_of_critical_gram_absorption` keeps a finite, potentially moving zero family. If the actual relative zero Gram has row-sum defect at most \(0\le\delta<1/2\) from \(\frac12I\), then
```math
E_M\le\frac{
 \mu_M+
 \displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{relativeCriticalSourceSq}(\gamma,N)}
 {(1/2-\delta)\psi(M)}
 +B_M}{1-\theta},
\qquad \psi(M)=\log L.
```
Its row-sum and absorption inequalities are explicit premises. This combines an existing zero-Gram energy upper estimate with a **new conditional complete-error upper estimate**. A bound on the certificate alone is not an upper bound on the complete error.

**Open:** prove the absorption premise for the canonical residual, or directly prove any unconditional \(R_M\to0\). This reduction does not make that arithmetic task easy or establish a convergence rate.

## 5. The actual reciprocal/displacement kernel

Module: [QMatchedGramScreening.lean](RH/QMatchedGramScreening.lean).

Let \(S,T\) be the matched legal subspaces in \(Q,P\). For the target \(\mathbf1\), put \(r=\mathbf1-\Pi_T^P\mathbf1\). Let \(K_{n,m}\in S\) be the Q-Riesz kernel for the evaluation difference, and let \(D\) multiply a physical sequence by \(1/n\). The existing screened kernel is
```math
W_{n,m}=(I-\Pi_T^P)D K_{n,m}.
```
Writing \(d_L=\Pi_S^Q\mathbf1-\Pi_T^P\mathbf1\), the existing matched identity is
```math
\zeta(2)\bigl(d_L(n)-d_L(m)\bigr)=\langle r,W_{n,m}\rangle_P.
```
For any test subspace \(U\) with projection \(\Pi_U\), define
```math
\mathcal B_{U,L}(n,m)=
 |\langle\Pi_Ur,\Pi_UW_{n,m}\rangle_P|
 +\|(I-\Pi_U)r\|_P\,\|(I-\Pi_U)W_{n,m}\|_P.
```
The new theorems prove
```math
\zeta(2)|d_L(n)-d_L(m)|
\le\mathcal B_{U,L}(n,m)
\le\|r\|_P\|W_{n,m}\|_P.
```
They also give the literal inverse-Gram squared-remainder formula for an independent finite family of actual vectors in the complete Pareto space.

The new `QFullOscillationDual.riemannHypothesis_of_head_split_pair_budget` replaces the older kernel-product premise by this no-larger split budget. It assumes nested cofinal divisor periods \(L_j\), expanding heads \(H_j\to\infty\), and a fixed positive modulus \(\ell\) and constant \(C\), such that
```math
\mathcal B_{U_j,L_j}(n,m)\le\zeta(2)C
\quad(1\le n,m\le H_j,\ n\equiv m\pmod\ell).
```
The existing projection limits and oscillation criterion then imply RH. The head bound remains **Open**, and a different bound for each head is insufficient.

## 6. Wolfram checks: external evidence only

It computed the actual finite P and Q normal equations, \(n=2,m=1\), and the first one or two positive critical zeros. ### Reproducible Wolfram calculation

```wl
ClearAll[calc];
calc[L_Integer, k_Integer] := Module[
 {ds, aa, p, q, A, Gp, Gq, fP, fQ, r, z, rows, Gz, proj, rem,
  beta, K, wd, wd2, w, h, x, old, folded, split, corr, tail, exact, n, m = 1},
 ds = Select[Divisors[L], # > 1 &]; aa = Range[L];
 p = N[(PolyGamma[(aa + 1)/L] - PolyGamma[aa/L])/L, 45];
 q = N[PolyGamma[1, aa/L]/(Zeta[2] L^2), 45];
 A = Table[Mod[a, d]/d, {a, aa}, {d, ds}];
 Gp = Transpose[A].DiagonalMatrix[p].A;
 Gq = Transpose[A].DiagonalMatrix[q].A;
 fP = A.LinearSolve[Gp, Transpose[A].p];
 fQ = A.LinearSolve[Gq, Transpose[A].q];
 r = 1 - fP; x = Sqrt[Most[p]] Most[r];
 z = N[Table[ZetaZero[j], {j, k}], 45];
 rows = Flatten[Table[
   With[{v = L^(-s) (Zeta[s, Most[aa]/L] - Zeta[s, (Most[aa] + 1)/L])/
       Sqrt[Most[p]]}, {Re[v], Im[v]}], {s, z}], 1];
 Gz = rows.Transpose[rows];
 proj = Transpose[rows].PseudoInverse[Gz, Tolerance -> 10^-35].rows;
 rem = x - proj.x; n = 2;
 beta = A[[n]] - A[[m]]; K = A.LinearSolve[Gq, beta];
 wd = (Zeta[2] q - p)/p;
 wd2 = (Zeta[3, aa/L]/L^3 - Zeta[2] q + p)/p;
 w = wd K - A.LinearSolve[Gp, Transpose[A].(p wd K)];
 h = Sqrt[Most[p]] Most[w];
 old = Sqrt[(p.r^2) *
   (p.(wd2 K^2) -
    (Transpose[A].(p wd K)).LinearSolve[Gp, Transpose[A].(p wd K)])];
 folded = Norm[x] Norm[h]; corr = (proj.x).(proj.h);
 tail = Norm[rem] Norm[h - proj.h]; split = Abs[corr] + tail;
 exact = Zeta[2] ((fQ - fP)[[n]] - (fQ - fP)[[m]]);
 <|"L" -> L, "zeros" -> k, "completeError" -> p.r^2,
   "zeroRemainder" -> rem.rem, "oldBound" -> old, "foldedBound" -> folded,
   "zeroScreenedBound" -> split, "signedCaptured" -> corr,
   "signedActual" -> exact, "pairingDefect" -> Abs[x.h - exact],
   "rowLegalDefect" ->
    Max[Abs[Flatten[rows.(DiagonalMatrix[Sqrt[Most[p]]].Most[A])]]]|>
];
N[Table[calc[L, k], {L, {6, 12, 60}}, {k, {1, 2}}], 10]
```

## 7. Published inputs and the remaining arithmetic task

- [Bagchi, On Nyman, Beurling and Báez-Duarte's Hilbert space reformulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607733): the discrete weighted approximation formulation.
- [Báez-Duarte, A strong version of the Nyman--Beurling criterion](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202141): the arithmetic approximation criterion.
- [Burnol, A lower bound in an approximation problem involving the zeros of the Riemann zeta function](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0103058): zero-test lower bounds and the finite-test strategy. A lower bound does not establish the upper estimate needed here.
- [Balazard and de Roton, Sur un critère de Báez-Duarte pour l'hypothèse de Riemann](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1689): the approximation upper estimate assumes RH; it cannot supply the missing unconditional input.
- [Bettin, Conrey and Farmer, An optimal choice of Dirichlet polynomials for the Nyman--Beurling criterion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5191): conditional asymptotics require RH and an additional reciprocal-zeta-derivative moment hypothesis.

The useful next target is one estimate on actual canonical objects:
1. Prove \(R_M\le\theta E_M+B_M\) with a fixed \(\theta<1\) and unconditional \(B_M\to0\); or
2. Prove a uniform expanding-head bound for the signed split budget; or
3. Produce legal competitors whose complete error tends to zero directly.

The first two are conditional reductions, not accomplished estimates. A fixed finite Gram calculation, a microscopic spacing limit, a DPP interpretation, or a CRT limit alone does not establish them. In particular, statistics of critical-line ordinates do not exclude an exceptional off-line zero.

All three new Lean modules are exported by `RiemannResearch.lean`.
